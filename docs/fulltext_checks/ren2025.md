## 1. Setting

- **Teacher** (eq. (1), p. 4):
  "f*(x) = Σ_{p=1}^P a_p σ(v*_p · x), ∀x ∈ R^d, where x ∼ γ := N(0, I_d) is the input, {v*_p}_{p∈[P]} ⊂ R^d are orthonormal with P ≫ 1, σ ∈ L²(γ) satisfies Assumption 2.1, and a_1 ≥ ··· ≥ a_P ≥ 0 are normalized so that Σ_p a_p² = 1. Since the input distribution and our learning algorithm are rotationally invariant, we may assume w.l.o.g. that v*_p = e_p ... While our scaling results will assume a_p follows a power law decay, no such assumptions are required for our optimization results."
  - The coefficients are **fixed, deterministic and non-negative**. There are no random or mean-zero coefficients.
  - Labels are **noiseless**: the dataset is "{(x_t, f*(x_t))}_{t∈N}" (p. 5).
  - P ≲ d^c for a small fixed c > 0 (informal theorem, p. 3).
- **Student** (eq. (2), p. 5):
  "f(x) := f(x; {v_k}_{k=1}^m) = Σ_{k=1}^m ‖v_k‖² σ(v̄_k · x), where {v_k} ⊂ R^d are trainable parameters and v̄_k := v_k/‖v_k‖. Note that this student network is parameterized to be 2-homogeneous in each v_k, i.e., the second-layer coefficients are coupled with the norm of the first-layer weights."
  - **The second layer is trained, but tied to the first-layer norm.** It is not fixed, not a separate free parameter, and not set by a schedule. Remark (p. 5): "when both layers are trained simultaneously under this parameterization, the growth of the second-layer norm ‖v_k‖ is coupled to the directional convergence of the first layer v̄_k, enabling an 'automatic deflation' process and making the single-phase training dynamics amenable to analysis. We believe that a similar proof strategy can be applied to simultaneous training of networks with decoupled second-layer weights."
  - Intro (p. 2): "we aim to characterize a natural, single-phase algorithm where both layers are updated simultaneously."
- **Loss**: per-sample MSE, l(x) = ½(f*(x) − f(x))² (eq. (3), p. 5). The population loss is the tensor-decomposition loss of eq. (4), p. 5:
  L = Σ_{i≥I} σ̂²_{2i} ( ‖a‖² − 2 Σ_p Σ_k a_p ‖v_k‖² ⟨v̄_k, v*_p⟩^{2i} + Σ_{k,l} ‖v_k‖² ‖v_l‖² ⟨v̄_k, v̄_l⟩^{2i} ). The "−2" and the absence of a ½ were re-typed from the garbled extraction.
- **Initialisation** (p. 5): "We initialize the student neurons v_k ∼ Unif(S^{d−1}(σ_0)), where σ_0 = 1/poly(d)". Theorem 2.1 uses "σ_0 = d^{−C}". So the second-layer scale at initialization is ‖v_k‖² = d^{−2C}, which is tiny.
- **Algorithm: online SGD, batch size 1, fresh sample each step** (p. 5): "Let {(x_t, f*(x_t))}_{t∈N} be our dataset with x_t i.i.d. ∼ N(0, I_d) being the fresh sample at step t. ... v_k(t+1) = v_k(t) − η ∇_{v_k} l(x_t), for all k ∈ [m]". Lemma D.1 (p. 61) calls the stochastic gradient a "mini-batch gradient", but the algorithm as stated uses one sample per step. Appendix C gives a separate population gradient-flow proof.
- **Learning rate**: Theorem 2.1 (p. 6) requires η ≲ [a_{min*} ‖a‖_1^{−2} m^{−1} P^{−1} δ*_P / log^C(md/δ*_P)] · min(Δ² d^{−I}, ε_D²). Discussion (p. 7): "Selecting η = Θ̃(a_min d^{−I} poly(m, P)), the runtime (and sample complexity) required to recover all directions ... is T = Θ̃(d^{2I−1} poly(P) a_min^{−2}) = d^{IE(σ)−1} P^{Θ(1)} ... Note that the d^{IE(σ)−1} factor matches the dimension scaling of online SGD for learning Gaussian single-index models [BAGJ21]."
  - So η ∝ d^{−k*/2}, the time unit is ηt ∼ d^{k*/2−1}, and the step count is T ∼ d^{k*−1}.
  - The informal theorem on p. 3 prints "n ≍ T ≍ a²_{P*} · d^{k*−1} poly(P)". The rendered PDF has the same text. It is a **typo** for a^{−2}: compare p. 7 and Corollary 2.3.
- **Simulations** (p. 8): population GD with fixed step, d = 2048, P = 1024, σ = h_4, β = 0.8, varying student width.

## 2. Assumptions on σ, and why k* = 2 and odd σ are excluded

- **Assumption 2.1 (Link function)** (p. 4): "Let {h_k}_{k∈N≥0} denote the normalized Hermite polynomials.
  (a) σ is even and has information exponent IE(σ) = 2I for I > 1, that is, the Hermite expansion of σ is given as σ = Σ_{i=I}^∞ σ̂_{2i} h_{2i}, and we require σ̂_{2I} ≥ c_σ; we also assume ‖σ‖_{L²(γ)} = 1, and ‖σ′‖_{L²(γ)}, ‖σ″‖_{L²(γ)} ≤ C_σ, where constants c_σ, C_σ > 0.
  (b) σ and σ′ have polynomial growth. That is, there exist universal constants C, Q > 0 such that |σ(x)| ∨ |σ′(x)| ≤ C(1 + x²)^{Q/2} for all x ∈ R."
  - **Consequence:** k* ∈ {4, 6, 8, ...}. The paper excludes k* = 2 (I = 1) and also every odd k*, including k* = 3, because σ is even. The abstract's "k* > 2" therefore means k* ≥ 4 and even.
- **Remark explaining the exclusions** (p. 5): "We focus on high information exponent IE(σ) > 2 link functions as in [OSSW24, SBH24, GWB25]. This setting entails that the learning of each single-index task is 'hard' in the sense that online SGD exhibits a long loss plateau, and we utilize this assumption to prove (approximate) decoupling of individual tasks. The condition on even σ simplifies the analysis by removing the 1/2 probability of neurons initialized in the wrong hemisphere (see e.g., [BAGJ21])."
- **Intro** (p. 2): "σ: R → R is an even activation function with information exponent k* > 2; this implies that (online) SGD learning of each task has an emergent learning curve with poly(d) initial plateau."
- **The only discussion of the k* = 2 case is in the Conclusion** (p. 15): "Finally, our analysis relies on high information exponent link functions to decouple the learning of different directions, which does not cover the case of IE(σ) = 2 studied in [MBB23, RL24] — for this setting, the scaling behavior for SGD training is studied in a companion work [BAEVW25] for the special case of quadratic activation function." [BAEVW25] is Ben Arous, Erdogdu, Vural, Wu, "Learning quadratic neural networks in high dimensions: SGD dynamics and scaling laws", 2025.
- **Technical reason, visible in the formulas** (not stated as a remark):
  - T_p contains the factor 1/(4I(I−1)), and Theorem C.1(c) has an exponent 1/(I−1). Both blow up at I = 1.
  - At I = 1 the overlap ODE dv̄²/dt ∝ a v̄^{2I} is linear. All directions then grow exponentially at once, with no sharp, separated escape times, so the greedy-selection and gap argument has nothing to work with.

## 3. Main theorems (verbatim, cleaned)

**Informal Proposition (scaling law)** (p. 3): "assuming a_p ≍ p^{−β} for β > 1/2, then (ignoring logarithmic factors) we have (a) Emergence. The p-th teacher neuron (where p ≲ m) is recovered at time ηt ∼ p^β d^{k*/2−1}. (b) Scaling law. The population squared error follows a power-law decay up to approximation barrier L(t) ∼ (tηd^{1−k*/2})^{(1−2β)/β} ∨ m^{1−2β}."

**Theorem 2.1 (Main theorem for online SGD)** (p. 6). Hypotheses in brief: C, C′ are large constants; σ_0 = d^{−C}; P* ∈ [P]; a_{min*} = min_{p∈[P*]} a_p; Δ ≃ δ*_P / (mP max(m,P)) = o_d(1). The requirements are
- d ≳ ‖a‖_1^4 Δ^{−8} a_{min*}^{−4} log^{8I} d;
- m ≳ P* log(P*/δ*_P) ∨ log(P/δ*_P);
- the bounds on ε_D, ε_R;
- the η bound from §1.

Conclusion (verbatim, cleaned): "With probability 1 − δ*_P, there exists an ordering of the student neurons v_1, ..., v_m and a mapping π: [P*] → [P] of student neurons to teacher neurons (see Equation (5)) such that, defining
T_p := 1 / (4I(I−1) σ̂²_{2I} a_{π(p)} η v̄^{2I−2}_{p,π(p)}(0)) ∀p ∈ [P*], and T_max := (1 + Δ/4) max_{p∈[P*]} T_p
we have:
(a) (Unused neurons). ‖v_k(t)‖² ≤ d^{−C′} =: σ_1² for all k > P*.
(b) (Convergence). v̄²_{p,π(p)}(t) ≥ 1 − ε_D and ‖v_p(t)‖² = a_{π(p)} ± ε_R for all p ∈ [P*], (1 + Δ/4)T_p ≤ t ≤ T_max.
(c) (Sharp Transition). v̄²_{p,π(p)}(t) ≤ d^{−1/2} and ‖v_p(t)‖² ≤ σ_1² for all p ∈ [P*], t ≤ (1 − Δ/256)T_p.
(d) (Loss Value). 1 − Σ_{p∈[P*]} a²_{π(p)} 1{t ≥ (1−Δ/4)T_p} − O(ε_D) ≤ L(t) ≤ 1 − Σ_{p∈[P*]} a²_{π(p)} 1{t ≥ (1+Δ/4)T_p} + O(ε_D)."

**Transition time.** v̄²(0) ≈ 1/d, so T_p ≍ d^{I−1}/(η a_{π(p)}) = d^{k*/2−1}/(η a_p). This is linear in 1/a_p. Theorem C.1 for gradient flow (p. 30) states it as "T_p := 1/(4I(I−1)σ̂²_{2I} a_{π(p)} v̄^{2I−2}_{p,π(p)}(0)) = Θ(1/(a_{π(p)} v̄^{2I−2}_{p,π(p)}(0))) = Θ̃(1/(a_{π(p)} d^{I−1}))". The last expression must be read as Θ̃(d^{I−1}/a_{π(p)}); the printed form is a typo. Appendix E (p. 94) writes v̄²(0) instead of v̄^{2I−2}(0), which is another typo; the two agree at I = 2. Discussion (p. 7): "a sharp transition in the learning of the teacher neuron v*_{π(p)} around time T_p ≃ (η a_{π(p)} ⟨v̄_p(0), v*_{π(p)}⟩^{2(I−1)})^{−1}."

**Proposition 2.2 (Scaling laws)** (p. 7): "suppose a_p = p^{−β}/Z where β > 1/2 and Z = Σ_{p=1}^P p^{−2β} is the normalizing constant. Then, with high probability,
(a) For p ≤ P* = Θ̃(m), the p-th teacher neuron v*_p is learned at time t = Θ̃(p^β d^{I−1} η^{−1}).
(b) There exist constants 0 < c_β < C_β and 0 < c′_β < C′_β that can depend only on β such that
c_β [ (m/log m)^{1−2β} + (K_0 η t / d^{I−1})^{(1−2β)/β} ] − O(ε_D) ≤ L(t) ≤ C_β [ (m/log m)^{1−2β} + (K_0 η t / d^{I−1})^{(1−2β)/β} ] + O(ε_D)
for all t ∈ [T_min, T_max], where K_0 := log^{2I−2} m / Z, T_min = C′_β d^{I−1}/(K_0 η) and T_max = c′_β P^β d^{I−1}/(K_0 η)."

**Scaling-law exponents:**
- Steps or samples at fixed η: L ∼ (ηt/d^{k*/2−1})^{−(2β−1)/β}.
- Width (parameters): L ∼ m^{1−2β}, up to logs. The approximation bottleneck is "governed by the width of the student network" (p. 7).
- β < 1/2 (p. 8): "L(t) = Θ̃((1 − (P/m)^{1−2β})_+ ∨ (1 − (tηd^{I−1})^{(1−2β)/β})_+)".
- **Compute-optimal** (p. 8): "given a fixed computational budget T ≍ mt, the compute-optimal model under constant learning rate exhibits the following scaling, L ∼ T^{(1−2β)/(1+β)}, m ∼ T^{1/(1+β)}". For β = 0.8 this gives L ∼ (mt)^{−1/3}.
- k* enters only through the time unit d^{k*/2−1} (and d^{k*−1} for samples). **The exponents depend only on β.**

**Corollary 2.3 (Unstable scaling law)** (p. 8): "Let m be the student network width and n be the total number of training examples. Then, there exists a choice of learning rate η (depending on n, m) such that with high probability the population loss after t = n steps of online SGD is L(n) = Θ̃( m^{1−2β} + (n / (d^{2I−1} poly(P)))^{(1−2β)/(2β)} ) ± O(ε_D)."
- This rests on choosing η = Θ̃(a_{π(p)} d^{−I}/poly(P)), which gives "T_p = Θ̃(a^{−2}_{π(p)} d^{2I−1} poly(P)) = Θ̃(p^{2β} d^{2I−1} poly(P))" (p. 8).
- "the above sample size scaling matches the minimax optimal rate for Gaussian sequence models ... the exponent is consistent with existing scaling law analyses of SGD on linear models [BAP24, LWK+24, PPXP24]."

## 4. Decoupling argument (greedy maximum selection)

- **Idealized dynamics** (Sec. 3.1, pp. 9-10, with σ = h_4): "d/dt v̄_1² ≈ 8a v̄_1⁴ prior to weak recovery ... closed-form solution: v̄_1²(t) = (1/v̄_1²(0) − 8at)^{−1}". The decoupled loss is L̃(t) = Σ_p a_p² 1{t < (8 a_p v̄²_{p,p}(0))^{−1}}. "Due to the 2-homogeneous parameterization, we can show that the norm of v will not grow until strong recovery is achieved, and the norm growth occurs at a much shorter timescale than the dynamics of v̄."
- **Greedy maximum selection, eq. (5)** (p. 11): "(π(1), v_1) := argmax_{q∈[P], v∈V} a_q v̄_q^{2I−2} ... (π(p+1), v_{p+1}) = argmax_{q∈[P]∖{π(1),...,π(p)}, v∈V∖{v_1,...,v_p}} a_q v̄_q^{2I−2}". "Following [BAGP24], we call (5) the greedy maximum selection scheme".
- **What is proved:**
  - Lemma 3.1 (p. 11): with probability 1 − O(δ_P) at initialization there are gaps.
    - (a) Row gap: a_{π(p)} v̄^{2I−2}_{p,π(p)} ≥ (1+δ_r) a_{π(q)} v̄^{2I−2}_{p,π(q)}.
    - (b) Column gap: v̄^{2I−2}_{p,π(p)} ≥ (1+δ_c) v̄^{2I−2}_{k,π(p)}.
    - (c) Threshold gap.
    - (d) Regularity: max_k ‖v̄_k‖²_∞ ≤ log² d/d, and so on.
    - Here δ_r = δ_P π/(2mP²) and δ_c = δ_t = δ_P π/(12m²P). These are 1/poly(P), not constants.
  - Theorem C.1 / Theorem 2.1 then show that **gradient flow and online SGD implement this greedy selection.** All "irrelevant" overlaps stay ≤ d^{−(1−γ)} (Induction Hypothesis C.2(b), p. 30). Each relevant overlap escapes at (1 ± o(1))T_p. After a direction is fitted it is removed from the residual ("automatic deflation"; Sec. 1.1, p. 3, and Sec. 3.2, p. 12).
- **Conditions:**
  - The heuristic (pp. 11-12) needs a_min P^{1/2} ≫ d^{−0.8} and a_min ≫ d^{−0.3}. Quote: "if a learner neuron is sufficiently random (or, more precisely, incoherent), then the influence of different teacher directions can be decoupled, and (ii) thanks to the sharp transitions in the training dynamics, when a large teacher direction gets fitted by a learner neuron, there is still enough randomness in the remaining learner neurons."
  - Formally the conditions are those of Theorem 2.1: d polynomially large in Δ^{−1} and a_{min*}^{−1}, m ≳ P* log P*, and the small η above.
  - Sharp transitions need IE > 2.
  - The SGD proof needs η ≲ d^{−2} δ_c² a_{π(q)} (for I = 2) so that SGD escape times match gradient-flow times up to a factor (1 + δ_c) (p. 13).
  - "Unstable discretization": η is scaled with a_{π(P*)}, not a_min (p. 14).
- **Caveat stated by the authors** (p. 4): "unlike these prior works, our problem setting does not imply that the learning of different tasks can be decoupled a priori, as student neurons may be attracted to multiple teacher directions and also interact with each other through the squared loss."

## 6. Limitations and future work (Conclusion, p. 15, complete)

"Our current results assume input data with identity covariance; one interesting extension is to consider anisotropic data x ∼ N(0, Σ) analogous to [MHWSE23, BQI25], and derive a two-parameter scaling law when the eigenvalues of Σ also follow a power law. Another future direction is to consider a decaying learning rate schedule that achieves the unstable scaling law (Corollary 2.3) at any time t. Finally, our analysis relies on high information exponent link functions to decouple the learning of different directions, which does not cover the case of IE(σ) = 2 studied in [MBB23, RL24] — for this setting, the scaling behavior for SGD training is studied in a companion work [BAEVW25] for the special case of quadratic activation function."

## 7. Overlap with our project

Our setting is the same additive teacher, learned **in context**: ŷ_q = Σ_j Γ_j σ(w_j·x_q) · (1/N) Σ_i y_i σ(w_j·x_i). The task coefficients c are random with mean zero. Our claims:
- the effective information exponent is 2k* with a fixed readout;
- it is 4k* with finite N and a trained readout;
- k* = 2 is easy in-weight (d log d) but hard in-context.

### (a) In-context version: **NOT CONTAINED**
- The paper has no context, no task distribution and no attention-like predictor.
- Its coefficients are deterministic and **non-negative** (a_1 ≥ ··· ≥ a_P ≥ 0). With mean-zero random c, the population in-weight gradient signal of this paper, which is linear in a_p, would vanish. So their setting cannot even express our task model.
- In the paper, "task" means "teacher neuron". This vocabulary clash may confuse reviewers, so we should state it explicitly.

### (b) Readout protocol changes the exponent: **NOT CONTAINED** (no contradiction; one caveat)
- Nothing in the paper says that training the second layer squares or doubles the exponent. Only one readout protocol appears: the trained, 2-homogeneous tied readout ‖v_k‖².
- In their model, k* enters the transition time as T_p ∝ 1/(η a_p v̄^{k*−2}(0)). The second-layer scale does not change the exponent.
- **Caveat a reviewer may raise.** Their second layer starts tiny: ‖v_k‖² = σ_0² = d^{−2C}. Yet the directional search is **not** slowed. Under the 2-homogeneous parameterization the tangent dynamics of v̄_k do not depend on ‖v_k‖ (Lemma B.1, p. 22-23: the tangent gradient divided by ‖v_k‖ involves only overlaps and the other neurons). The norm grows only after strong recovery (p. 10).
  - So "a small, trained readout" by itself does **not** raise the exponent in-weight.
  - Our 4k* claim must therefore rest on the in-context structure: the readout multiplies a context-estimated correlation whose task average vanishes, plus the finite-N noise term. It cannot rest on the readout being trained from small initialization as such.
  - We should check whether a balanced or 2-homogeneous parameterization of (Γ_j, w_j) would remove our 2k* → 4k* doubling, and say why it does or does not.
  - Their remark that the proof should extend to "decoupled second-layer weights" (p. 5) is a statement about proof technique, not about exponents.
- **Weighting by a_p vs. a_p²** (our derivation, not in the paper). Their signal is linear in a_p, which gives emergence at p^β. If our in-context signal is weighted by E[c_p²] ∝ a_p², the analogue of Prop. 2.2 gives t_p ∝ p^{2β} and L ∼ t^{−(2β−1)/(2β)}. That coincides with the exponent of their Corollary 2.3 ("unstable" law), though the mechanism differs. Worth stating, to pre-empt a "same exponent as Ren et al." objection.

### (c) k* = 2 regime: **NOT CONTAINED**
- k* = 2 is **explicitly excluded** (Assumption 2.1, I > 1; Remark p. 5; Conclusion p. 15). Odd k*, including 3, is also excluded because σ is even.
- They defer IE = 2 to [MBB23, RL24] and to the companion paper [BAEVW25] on quadratic activations. **[BAEVW25] should get its own full-text check** before we claim novelty for "k* = 2 in-weight is easy, d log d". It covers the in-weight IE = 2 scaling laws, though presumably not in-context.
- Nothing in the paper contradicts "k* = 2 hard in-context".
- **Useful for us:** their decoupling machinery needs effective IE > 2 to get sharp, separated transitions. In-context with k* = 2, our claimed effective exponent is 2k* = 4 (or 4k* = 8). That puts the problem inside the sharp-transition regime where their greedy-selection and deflation argument applies. Their proof is a candidate template for a rigorous in-context result, and a candidate source of the "emergence + scaling law" in-context story.
- Our in-weight baseline for general k* should cite BAGJ21 (d^{k*−1} samples) rather than Ren et al., whose results hold only for even k* ≥ 4.

### (d) Context length as a resource: **NOT CONTAINED**
- Their resources are SGD steps / samples (n = t, batch size 1), student width m (parameters) and compute T ≍ mt.
- There is no context length and no per-task sample size. Their dataset is a single stream of fresh (x_t, f*(x_t)) pairs from one fixed target.
