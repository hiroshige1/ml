## Verdicts (summary)

| Our claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| k*=2 in-weight learning of a direction needs ~d log d samples | **SUPPORTED.** For r=1 (phase retrieval) and for the light-tailed case they give n ≍ T ≃ d polylog(d), and they state explicitly that k=2 costs an extra log d over d (p. 4, p. 8, p. 9). Their Theorem 2 gives d·log^{Cα+1} d (Cα is unspecified), which is not exactly d log d. The plain "d log d" attribution belongs to [BAGJ21]/[TV23], which BAEVW cite for it. |
| In-context version needs d³ (fixed readout); "in-weight easy, in-context hard" | **NOT CONTAINED.** There is no ICL, context length, task distribution or transformer anywhere. No contradiction. |
| M=64 > d=32 in-weight student fits the quadratic target collectively (pooled alignment → 1, no single neuron aligned) | **NOT COVERED as a theorem** (they always have r_s < d), but **CONSISTENT** in spirit. Individual-neuron alignment is uninformative by the O(r_s) symmetry, so they define alignment at the subspace/Gram level. The dynamics depend on W only through the Gram matrix WWᵀ (an autonomous matrix Riccati ODE), and neuron-wise alignment needs a separate "fine-tuning" rotation. Caveat: their subspace alignment ‖Uᵀθ_j‖² would be trivially 1 at init when r_s ≥ d. Our weighted q_p is the analogue of their *weight* Gram G_W, not of G_U. |
| Decoupling across directions for k=2 | They say explicitly that the k ≥ 3 decoupling **does not hold** for k=2 (pp. 3, 12). They get sequential, decoupled-*looking* emergence (one transition per direction at time ∝ 1/λ_j) only via Riccati monotonicity and comparison to diagonal (decoupled) systems. |

## 1. Setting

- **Teacher** (eq. (2.1), p. 4): "y = f∗(x) + ϵ with f∗(x) := (1/∥Λ∥_F) Σ_{j=1}^r λ_j (⟨θ_j, x⟩² − 1) and x ∼ N(0, I_d), where ... ϵ is zero-mean, independent sub-Gaussian noise; r is the teacher network width; and {θ_j} ⊂ R^d is an orthonormal set of unknown signal vectors ... λ1 > λ2 > ··· > λr > 0 with a power-law decay λ_j ≍ j^{−α} for α ≥ 0 ... We focus on the regime where r ≍ d^β for β ∈ (0, 1)." The coefficients are fixed, deterministic and **positive**, so ΘΛΘᵀ is PSD. Remark 1 (p. 5): orthogonality is w.l.o.g. because f∗ ∝ Tr(ΘΛΘᵀxxᵀ) + cst, via the spectral decomposition.
- **Student** (eq. (2.2), p. 5): "ŷ(x, W) = (1/√r_s) Σ_{j=1}^{r_s} (⟨w_j, x⟩² − ∥w_j∥²_2) ... We may equivalently write the student network as ŷ(x, W) = (1/√r_s) Σ ∥w_j∥²_2 · (⟨w̄_j, x⟩² − 1) where w̄_j is unit-norm; **since our student does not have trainable second-layer, the norm component ∥w_j∥²_2 allows the model to adapt to the target second-layer λ_j**; this homogeneous parameterization has been studied in prior works [CB20, GRWZ21]."
  - This is the same 2-homogeneous "norm acts as readout" parameterisation as our B-tied student, where q_p = Σ_j |u_j|² m_jp² / a_p.
- **Layers trained:** only the first layer W. There is no separate second layer, and the effective readout is ∥w_j∥². In the SGD analysis (Algorithm 1, p. 7) the directional part is trained by online SGD on the Stiefel manifold (polar retraction, so the norms are frozen). A final "fine-tuning" step then fits W_t Ω by ERM over N_Ft ≍ r_s² log⁵ d fresh samples: "this mirrors the layer-wise training paradigm commonly used in theoretical analyses" (p. 7).
- **Loss:** squared loss with prefactor 1/16. Population risk (2.3), p. 5: R(W) = (1/8)‖(1/√r_s)WWᵀ − (1/‖Λ‖_F)ΘΛΘᵀ‖²_F + E[ϵ²]/16.
- **Init:** GF uses W_0,ij ~ N(0, 1/d) (p. 5). SGD uses W_0 uniform on the Stiefel manifold (Thm 2).
- **Width regimes** (eq. (3.1), p. 6): for α ∈ [0, 0.5), r/r_s → φ ∈ (0, ∞). For α > 0.5, r_s ≍ 1. The abstract (p. 1) gives the student width as r_s ≍ d^γ, γ ∈ [0, 1) (p. 3: "r ≍ d^β for β ∈ (0, 1) and r_s ≍ d^γ for γ ∈ [0, 1)"). **The student width is always ≪ d.** The case r_s ≥ d is never treated, and the Stiefel constraint requires r_s ≤ d.
- **Learning rate** (Thm 2, p. 8): η ≍ (1/d)·1/(r^α log^{Cα}(1+d/r_s)) for α ∈ [0, 0.5), and η ≍ (1/d)·1/log^{Cα} d for α > 0.5. Table 1 notes (p. 4): "η ∼ 1/(d polylog(d))" (light-tailed) and "η ∼ 1/(d r^α polylog(d))" (heavy-tailed).
- **Experiments:** Fig. 1(b) (p. 2): "online SGD with batch size d on squared loss. We set d = 3200, and for the teacher model r = 2400, α = 1." App. A (pp. 20-21), for the ReLU / He1+He2 figures: "d = 5000, number of teacher neurons r = 2400, student widths r_s ∈ {32, 64, 128, 256, 512}, and learning rate η = 0.5/√r", population GD.

## 2. Main results (k* = 2, quadratic)

- **Plateau length** (p. 1): "The quadratic σ has information exponent k = 2 ... randomly initialized parameters are close to a saddle point in high dimensions; hence the SGD dynamics exhibit a plateau ("search" phase) of length log d before the loss decreases sharply ("descent" phase)."
- **Theorem 1 (population GF, p. 6).** Effective timescale (definition from D.3, p. 27; the main-text rendering is garbled): T_eff := κ_eff √r_s ∥Λ∥_F log(d/r_s), with κ_eff = r^α for α < 0.5 and 1 for α > 0.5. Effective width r_eff = ⌊r_s(1 − log^{−1/8} d) ∧ r⌋ (α < 0.5) or r_s (α > 0.5).
  - "1. Alignment: For j ≤ r_eff ... A(t T_eff, θ_j) = 1{t ≥ 1/λ_j} + o_d(1)." (3.2)
  - "2. Risk curve: R(t T_eff) = 1 − (1/∥Λ∥²_F) Σ_{j=1}^{r_eff} λ_j² 1{t ≥ 1/λ_j} + o_d(1)." (3.3)
  - In words: direction j escapes **sharply** at GF time ≈ √r_s ‖Λ‖_F log(d/r_s) / λ_j (times r^α in the heavy-tailed case). These are sequential emergences, a log-d plateau scaled by 1/λ_j.
  - Remark 2 (p. 6): "Theorem 1 verifies the additive model hypothesis [MLGT24] for quadratic neural networks in the feature learning regime; specifically, (3.2) identifies sharp transition time in alignment between student weights and the j-th teacher direction, and (3.3) suggests that the cumulative loss can be decomposed into individual emergent risk curves where the timescale is decided by the signal strength λ_j."
- **Scaling laws.** Corollary 1 (p. 6) and Corollary 2 (p. 9, SGD with ηt → t_c):
  - Heavy-tailed (α ∈ [0, 0.5)): R(t r log d) → (1 − C t^{(1−2α)/α})_+ ∨ (1 − φ^{1−2α})_+.
  - Light-tailed (α > 0.5): R(t log d) → Θ(t^{−(2α−1)/α} + r_s^{−(2α−1)}).
  - So the **time/data exponent is (2α−1)/α** and the **width exponent is 2α−1** (Table 1, p. 4). Fig. 4 caption (p. 12): "the exponent (dashed lines) nearly matches our theoretical prediction for the quadratic setting (1−2α)/α".
- **Theorem 2 (online SGD on Stiefel, p. 8).** "1. Runtime and sample complexity: If T ≥ d r^{1+α} log^{Cα+1}(1 + d/r_s) (α ∈ [0, 0.5)), d log^{Cα+1} d (α > 0.5), we have R(T) = R_opt + o_d(1)." It reproduces the same alignment and risk step curves in the variable ηt.
- **Sample complexity** (Remark 4, p. 8): "n ≍ T ≃ d r^{1+α} polylog(1 + d/r_s) in the heavy-tailed case, and T ≃ d polylog(d) in the light-tailed case ... In the light-tailed regime (α > 1/2), the required sample size n ≃ d polylog(d) is information theoretically optimal up to logarithmic factors. Note that kernel methods and neural networks in the lazy regime [JGH18, COB19] requires n ≳ d² samples to learn a quadratic target function". For α ∈ (0, 1/2) they conjecture the optimum is T ≃ dr.
  - Isotropic (α = 0), p. 9: "n ≍ T ≍ dr polylog(1 + d/r_s). This interpolates between the n ≃ d polylog(d) rate for phase retrieval r = 1 [TV23, BAGJ21], and n ≃ d² as r → d, which matches the sample complexity in the linear-width regime [MTM+24, ETZK25]." This improves on [RL24]'s d·poly(r).
  - p. 4 (related work): "while this model has d parameters to be estimated, **the quadratic link (with information exponent k = 2) incurs an additional log d factor in the complexity of online SGD**."
  - **Answer to "d log d, d, or d²?":** one direction (or O(1) effective directions) costs **d·polylog d**. It is d·log d in the cited [BAGJ21]/[TV23] sense; their own bound has log^{Cα+1} d. r directions cost d·r·polylog. Full linear width costs d². d² is also the lazy/kernel cost.
- **Decoupling** (p. 3): "[OSSW24, SBH24] assumed high information exponent k > 2, to decouple the dynamics into r independent single-index models, but such property does not hold in our quadratic case (k = 2). We address this by leveraging the closed-form solution of the quadratic problem [MBB23], which satisfies a Matrix Riccati ODE." Conclusion (p. 12): "Link functions with higher information exponent is studied in a companion work [RNWL25], where the precise risk scaling is established by exploiting a decoupling structure that is unique to the information exponent k > 2 setting."
  - Proposition 1 (p. 9): the weight Gram G_W = WWᵀ and the alignment Gram G_U = ΘᵀUUᵀΘ each follow an autonomous matrix Riccati ODE. Proof idea (p. 10): "If G_0 is diagonal, the solution remains diagonal ... reducing the dynamics to independent scalar ODEs ... For general G_0, we construct diagonal matrices G0+ ⪰ G0 ⪰ G0− . By monotonicity, the corresponding trajectories upper and lower bound {G(t)} ... as d → ∞, their trajectories converge to the same limit." Decoupling therefore holds only asymptotically and at the Gram-matrix level, not neuron by neuron.
- **Width > d / collective fits:** never treated. The closest statements are below.
  - p. 5: "the student network is invariant to right-multiplication of its weight matrix by an orthonormal matrix ... Consequently, **any notion of alignment that depends on individual directions in W may not be informative**. To capture directional learning in a way that respects this symmetry, we define alignment in terms of the subspace spanned by the student weights ... Alignment(W, θ_j) := ∥Uᵀθ_j∥²_2" (with W = UQ^{1/2}, the polar decomposition).
  - p. 7: "Since the goal is to ensure subspace alignment as in (2.5), **the overlap of individual student-teacher weights is not relevant during this phase**." Then (p. 8): "we perform a fine-tuning step to rotate W_t so that each w_j aligns with the corresponding teacher direction θ_j."
  - Footnote 1 (p. 5): "The definition in (2.5) may fail to converge to 1 when α = 0 and r_s < r, due to rotational symmetry in the teacher network." In that case they use eigenvalues of ΘᵀUUᵀΘ instead.
  - Remark 3 (p. 8): "We conjecture that a standard Euclidean discretization of (GF) can also achieve the same risk scaling; see Figure 1(b) for empirical evidence."

## 3. In-context learning / context length / task distributions / readout

- Grep hits for "context" are only "In this context" (p. 2) and "when clear from the context" (pp. 5, 5). "transformer" appears only in reference titles. There is **no** ICL, context length, prompt, or distribution over tasks. A "task" (Fig. 1(a), p. 2) means one fixed teacher component λ_j σ(⟨θ_j, x⟩) under the "additive model hypothesis [MLGT24, NFLL24]".
- **Readout:** there is no trainable second layer. The readout is absorbed into ∥w_j∥² (2-homogeneous). In the SGD algorithm the norms are frozen by the Stiefel step and then fitted by a separate fine-tuning ERM (N_Ft ≍ r_s² log⁵ d). There is no fixed-readout vs trained-readout comparison and no exponent change due to readout.

## 4. Overlap / contradiction with our claims

1. **"k*=2 in-weight needs d log d (Ben Arous et al. 2021)."** Consistent. BAEVW cite [BAGJ21, TV23] for n ≃ d polylog(d) at r = 1 and state that k = 2 adds a log d factor (p. 4). Suggested wording: "≈ d log d (d polylog d)". If we want the multi-direction statement, BAEVW give d·polylog d (α > 1/2) or d r polylog (α = 0) for the extensive-width in-weight problem with power-law λ_j. That is a stronger and more recent citation than BAGJ21 for "in-weight is easy".
2. **"in-context needs d³ (fixed readout); in-weight easy, in-context hard."** Not addressed. BAEVW has nothing in-context, so it neither supports nor contradicts the d³. Note that BAEVW's d² figure is the in-weight linear-width (r → d) cost and the lazy/kernel cost (p. 8, p. 9), not an in-context cost. Do not cite BAEVW for anything ICL-related. They are useful only as the in-weight baseline.
3. **"M=64 > d=32 ⇒ collective fit, pooled alignment → 1, no single neuron aligned."**
   - BAEVW's theorems require r_s ≪ d, so they **do not cover** M > d. We should not claim they predict or prove our observation.
   - The framework is consistent with it: their whole analysis runs through G_W = WWᵀ (an autonomous Riccati ODE, Prop. 1, p. 9) and treats individual-neuron alignment as uninformative (p. 5, p. 7). A neuron-wise match is obtained only by an extra rotation (fine-tuning, p. 8).
   - **Caveat on metrics.** BAEVW's alignment ∥Uᵀθ_j∥² (projection onto col(W)) is identically 1 whenever W spans R^d, i.e. generically at init when r_s ≥ d. So it would not detect anything in our M = 64 > d = 32 setting. Our q_p = Σ_j |u_j|² m_jp² / a_p = θ_pᵀ(WWᵀ)θ_p / a_p is the diagonal of their weight Gram G_W in the teacher basis (up to the 1/√r_s and ‖Λ‖_F normalisations). If we cite them, we should say our pooled alignment is the G_W analogue.
   - **Inference (ours, not stated in the paper):** in Lemma 2 (p. 23) / D.3 (p. 27) the escape timescale carries log(d/r_s), which comes from G_W(0) having eigenvalues ~ r_s/d on an r_s-dimensional subspace. When r_s ≥ d, G_W(0) ≈ (r_s/d) I is already full rank and O(1), so the log-d search plateau disappears. That fits our exp-2 observation that B at M > d is "essentially already fit at init (q_p(0) ~ 1) so there is no emergence event". This suggests the collective fit is a width-≥-d effect, as our README_Btied conjectures. A run with M < d would test it.
4. **Decoupling.** If our text says k = 2 learning "decouples across directions", BAEVW explicitly says the k ≥ 3 decoupling "does not hold in our quadratic case (k = 2)" (p. 3). They nevertheless prove per-direction sharp emergence at time ∝ 1/λ_j (Thm 1) and an additive-model risk curve, via Riccati monotonicity. Be precise: the emergence times look decoupled, but the mechanism is coupled matrix dynamics. That matches our README_Btied verdict ("timescale law looks decoupled, mechanism is pooled").
