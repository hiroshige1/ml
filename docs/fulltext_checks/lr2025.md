# Full-text check: Tsiolis, Mousavi-Hosseini, Erdogdu (2025)

**"From Information to Generative Exponent: Learning Rate Induces Phase Transitions in SGD"**, arXiv:2510.21020v1 (23 Oct 2025), 28 pages (main text pp. 1-10, refs pp. 11-13, appendices A-D pp. 14-28).
Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.21020. The full text was read, including every appendix statement (A.1-A.4, B.1-B.10, C.1-C.4, D).
Page numbers are PDF page numbers. Quotes come from the pypdf text extraction; math was re-typed where the extraction garbled it.

## Verdicts (summary)

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| (a) Readout at its optimum a* ∝ E[y σ(w·x)] gives the effective landscape g(m)² and doubles the effective exponent (in-weight analogue of C3, 2k* → 4k*; "trained readout from small init gives exponent 2k*") | **NOT CONTAINED** |
| (b) Readout shrinks because of label or estimation noise, and that slows feature learning | **NOT CONTAINED** |
| (c) In-context learning, transformers, context length, finite-sample noise in the readout | **NOT CONTAINED** |

The paper's second-layer mechanism runs the other way from (a). A large second-layer learning rate **helps**: it brings in a non-correlational y² term whose exponent is IE(σ*²) = p₂ ≤ p. Nothing in the paper replaces a with its population optimum, so the paper has no slowdown or doubling result.

## 1. Setting

- **Target: Gaussian single-index model** (Sec. 2, p. 4, eq. 2.1):
  "y_i = σ*(⟨x_i, θ*⟩) + ζ_i, x_i ~ i.i.d. N(0, I_d), where θ* ∈ S^{d−1} is the ground truth direction, σ* : R → R is a (nonlinear) link function, and ζ_i is i.i.d. symmetric sub-Weibull label noise with O(1) tail parameter."
- **Student: two-layer network** (p. 4, eq. 2.2):
  "f(x; W, a, b) = (1/N) Σ_j a_j σ_j(⟨x, w_j⟩ + b_j)". It has "first-layer weights w_j ∈ S^{d−1}, biases b_j ∈ R, second layer weights a_j ∈ R, and polynomial activations σ_j".
- **Goal: weak recovery** (p. 4): "starting from a uniform initialization on the sphere S^{d−1} where ⟨θ, w_j^(0)⟩ ≍ d^{−1/2} ... we seek T such that ⟨θ*, w_j^(T)⟩ ≳ 1/polylog d."
- **Exponents**: p = IE(σ*), p_i := IE(σ*^i), p* = GE(σ*) (Defs. 2.1-2.2, p. 4).
- **Loss**: correlation loss, not squared loss (p. 5): "the standard squared loss ... and the correlation loss ℓ(y,y′) = 1 − yy′ induce identical gradients in the population limit, and are known to produce similar dynamics under small initialization of the second layer (see e.g., [AAM23, LOSW24]). Therefore, in this paper we examine choices of the oracle ψ_η based on the correlation loss." This is the only mention of small second-layer initialization.
- **Generic update** (eq. 3.1, p. 5):
  w^(t+1) ← w^(t) + γ ψ_η(y^(t), ⟨x^(t), w^(t)⟩) P⊥_{w^(t)} x^(t), then normalize. γ is the "global" (first-layer) learning rate. η "control[s] the scale of any non-correlational terms in the oracle ψ" (p. 5). The key coefficients are (eq. 3.2) μ_i(η) := E_{(a,b)~N(0,I₂)}[ψ_η(σ*(a), b) He_i(a) He_{i−1}(b)].
- **Alternating SGD (Algorithm 2, p. 8): which layers train, and at what learning rates**
  - "Initialize w^(0) ~ Unif(S^{d−1}), a = 1"
  - "Update ã^(t+1) ← a + η y σ(⟨x, w^(t)⟩)"
  - "Update w^(t+1) ← w^(t) + γ y ã^(t+1) σ′(⟨x, w^(t)⟩) P⊥_{w^(t)} x"
  - Normalize w.
  - The second-layer step is a **single-sample gradient step from a = 1 (O(1) initialization, not small)**. It is **not persisted** (App. C.3, p. 24): "Note that we only use the second layer update ã in order to update the first layer parameters w. We do not replace the second layer parameter with ã at the subsequent iteration, but instead keep a. For simplicity, assume a = 1."
  - In effect only the first layer is trained. The second layer gets a fresh lookahead step on each sample, with learning rate η ≲ 1. γ is set as in Cor. 4.3.
  - The second layer is trained only at the end, by ridge regression after strong recovery (App. B.7, Prop. B.10, quoted from [LOSW24, Lemma 20]).
- **Numerical experiments** (App. D, pp. 26-27): noiseless σ* = σ = He₃, N = 1, "f(x) = aσ(⟨x,w⟩). The network is initialized with a = 1", d ∈ {25, 50, 75}, batch size B = 128, one pass.

## 2. Main results (verbatim, cleaned)

**Theorem 3.3 (p. 6).** "Suppose Assumptions 3.1 and 3.2 hold. Let w^(0) ∈ S^{d−1} such that ⟨θ*, w^(0)⟩ ≍ d^{−1/2}. Then, there exists C ≳ 1/polylog d such that for any δ ∈ (0,1), if γ ≤ Cδ max_{1≤i≤r} μ_i d^{−(i/2 ∨ 1)}, then
T(η) = min_{1≤i≤r, μ_i>0} Θ̃( γ^{−1} (μ_i(η))^{−1} d^{(i−2)/2 ∨ 0} )   (3.3)
iterations of (3.1) are necessary and sufficient to achieve ⟨θ*, w⟩ ≳ 1/polylog d with probability at least 1 − δ."

**Remark 3.4 (p. 6).** With the optimal γ ≍ max_i μ_i(η) d^{−(i/2 ∨ 1)}, the result becomes "T(η) = min_{1≤i≤r, μ_i>0} Θ̃((μ_i(η))^{−2} d^{(i−1)∨1})" (3.4).

**Corollary 4.1 (online SGD, p. 7).** μ_i = i u_i(σ*) u_i(σ). "Θ̃(d^{(p−1)∨1}) iterations of the update (4.1) are necessary and sufficient to achieve weak recovery."

**Corollary 4.2 (batch reuse SGD, p. 8).** μ_i(η) ≍ Σ_k (ηd)^{k−1}/(k−1)! · u_{i−1}(σ^{(k)}(σ′)^{k−1}) u_i(σ*^k) (eq. 4.4). The result: "T(η) = min_{1≤i≤r} Θ̃((ηd)^{−2(i−1)} d^{(p_i−1)∨1})" (4.5). The phase transition is at η ≤ d^{([(p_j−1)∨1] − [(p_i−1)∨1]) / (2(j−i)) − 1} (4.6). With η ≲ d^{−(p+1)/2} the paper recovers online SGD. With η ≳ d^{−1} and r ≥ I it gets T = Θ̃(d).

**Alternating SGD oracle (eq. 4.7-4.8, p. 8):**
"ψ(y,z) = y a σ′(z) + η y² σ(z) σ′(z)", "μ_i(η) = a i u_i(σ*) u_i(σ) + η u_{i−1}(σσ′) u_i(σ*²)."
"The first term is identical to what emerges from the vanilla online SGD update (4.1). The second term arises from the non-correlational update. Note that if η is chosen too small, the latter term may not dominate even when p₂ < p."

**Corollary 4.3 (alternating SGD, p. 9), verbatim.** "Assume μ_p, μ_{p₂} > 0, η ≲ 1, γ ≍ max{d^{−(p/2∨1)}, η d^{−(p₂/2∨1)}}, and w^(0) ∈ S^{d−1} such that ⟨θ*, w^(0)⟩ ≍ d^{−1/2}. Then, with high probability,
T(η) = Θ̃(d^{(p−1)∨1}) ∧ Θ̃(η^{−2} d^{(p₂−1)∨1})   (4.9)
iterations of Algorithm 2 are necessary and sufficient to achieve weak recovery."
The phase transition (eq. 4.10, for p ≥ 2) is at "η ≍ d^{−½[(p−p₂)∨(p−2)]}". For σ* = He₃ (p = 3, p₂ = 2) the transition is at η ≍ d^{−1/2}: "At or below this threshold, alternating SGD has quadratic complexity, while η ≳ 1/polylog d gives linear complexity."

**Deep extension (eq. 4.11, p. 9; App. C.4, eqs. C.21-C.22, pp. 25-26).** μ_i(η) ≍ Σ_{j=1}^{D} η^{j−1} u_i(σ*^j), and T = max_{1≤i≤D} Θ̃(η^{−2(i−1)} d^{(p_i−1)∨1}). For σ(z) = z², three layers and u₂(σ*³) > 0, weak recovery takes Θ̃(d) iterations when η ≍ 1 (footnote 5, p. 9). The positivity of the Hermite coefficients is assumed: "these assumptions are difficult to verify in general."

**Strong recovery and readout (App. B.6-B.7, pp. 21-23).** Prop. B.7 gives strong recovery in T′ = min_i Θ̃(γ^{−1} ε^{−1} μ_i^{−1}). Remark B.8: "achieving weak recovery is the bottleneck during training." Prop. B.10 (ridge regression on a, from [LOSW24, Lemma 20]) uses Θ̃(N^{−4} + ε^{−4}) samples.

## 3. Mechanism: why a large second-layer learning rate acts like y → y²

- p. 8: "Crucially, the same sample (x,y) is used in these two updates. This produces a similar effect to batch reuse SGD without the need to apply consecutive gradient updates to W. Specifically, the update to W in alternating SGD contains the label transformation y ↦ y². This leads to a reduction in sample complexity if p₂ := IE(σ*²) < p and the second layer learning rate η is sufficiently large."
- The mechanism works one sample at a time. ã = 1 + η y σ(⟨x,w⟩) uses the **same** sample's y. Multiplying by the first-layer gradient factor y σ′ gives η y² σσ′. In expectation this involves the Hermite coefficients of σ*², so the relevant exponent is IE(σ*²) = p₂, and p₂ can be **smaller** than p.
- p. 5: "if the update for w_j contains the monomial transformation y^I = (σ*(⟨x,θ*⟩))^I, the sample complexity can be reduced to depend on p* instead of p. ... this will only occur if the scaling of the y^I term (as determined by the learning rates) is sufficiently large. Otherwise, we fall back into the information exponent regime."
- Link to two-timescale work (p. 8): "Algorithm 2 is related to studies on a two-timescales optimization dynamics of two-layer networks [BMZ24, MB23, BBPV23, WMHC24, BPV25], where training the second layer at a faster timescale simplifies the analysis. However, unlike these works, we perform sequential gradient updates on the two layers, and use the different learning rate scales to obtain a better sample complexity."

## 4. Checks against our claims

### (a) A readout at its optimum doubles the exponent: NOT CONTAINED

- The paper never sets a to its optimum a* ∝ E[yσ(w·x)]. It never computes a population-averaged readout as a function of the overlap m. It never writes an effective landscape g(m)². It never states that the information exponent doubles (p → 2p, or 2k* → 4k*).
- Its readout is a **single-sample, non-persisted** step from a = 1 (Alg. 2; C.3 quote above). The useful term is y² from the same sample, not (E[yσ])·y. If the readout were replaced by its population mean, the y² term would disappear and the paper's speed-up would go with it. The paper does not discuss this contrast.
- Initialization is a = 1 (O(1)), not small. The only small-init remark is the p. 5 comment that squared and correlation loss behave alike "under small initialization of the second layer". That remark is the reason the paper uses the correlation loss with a held fixed. It is not a claim about a readout growing like m^p.
- So the in-weight analogue ("trained readout from small init makes the effective exponent 2k*") is **not stated in this paper**. The paper's only pointer in that direction is the citation list for two-timescale dynamics: [BMZ24] Berthier-Montanari-Zhou, "Learning time-scales in two-layers neural networks"; [MB23] Marion-Berthier; [BBPV23] Bietti-Bruna-Pillaud-Vivien, "On learning Gaussian multi-index models with gradient flow"; [WMHC24]; [BPV25] Barboni-Peyré-Vialard. Those papers, especially BMZ24 and BBPV23 where the second layer is fast or optimal, are the places to check for the in-weight analogue. This paper is not.

### (b) Readout shrinkage from noise slows feature learning: NOT CONTAINED

- Label noise ζ is handled only as a sub-Weibull nuisance. The coefficients are redefined with noise, μ_i := E_{a,b} E_ζ[ψ_η(σ*(a)+ζ, b) He_i(a) He_{i−1}(b)] (eq. B.11, p. 17). For alternating SGD, the noise terms drop out of μ_i (eq. C.13, p. 24):
  "μ_i = i u_i(σ)u_i(σ*) + η u_{i−1}(σσ′) E_ζ E_a[(σ*²(a) + 2ζσ*(a) + ζ²) He_i(a)] = i u_i(σ)u_i(σ*) + η u_{i−1}(σσ′) u_i(σ*²)."
  ζ is symmetric, so the 2ζσ* term vanishes, and ζ² is constant, so He_i kills it for i ≥ 1. Noise therefore does not shrink anything; it only enters the martingale noise ν^(t) (Lemma B.4, eq. B.12; Prop. B.5 proof).
- The paper has no shrinkage factor of the form signal/(signal + noise/N). N is only the number of hidden neurons; the paper has no context-length N. The readout is fixed at a = 1 during feature learning, so it cannot shrink. Finite-sample estimation of the readout appears only in the post-hoc ridge step (Prop. B.10), and that step does not feed back into feature learning.
- What does slow learning in the paper is a **small learning rate η**, not noise: "if η is chosen too small, the latter term may not dominate even when p₂ < p" (p. 8). The analogy is loose. A small η scales the useful term down, which plays the role of a shrunken multiplier. But the paper attributes it to the hyperparameter, not to noise. Even then it only changes the prefactor (η^{−2}) and which exponent wins (p versus p₂). It never doubles an exponent.

### (c) In-context learning, transformers, context length: NOT CONTAINED

- The extracted text contains no occurrences of "context", "in-context", "transformer", "attention", "prompt" or "token". "Sequence" appears only in the math sense (Lemmas A.3-A.4, eq. 5.4).
- Finite-sample noise in the readout: none, apart from the ridge-regression sample count in Prop. B.10.

## 5. Other points useful for positioning

- The paper frames the second-layer learning rate as a **hyperparameter that selects the query class** (CSQ-like → SQ-like). It does not treat the readout's *value* as a multiplier on the feature gradient.
- For the Theorem 3.3 framework to apply, μ_i(η) must be **independent of the current overlap** apart from the explicit κ^{i−1} power. A readout that depends on m (such as our Γ*(m) or a*(m)) does not fit their oracle class (3.1) without adding state. That gap is consistent with the "NOT CONTAINED" verdict for (a).
