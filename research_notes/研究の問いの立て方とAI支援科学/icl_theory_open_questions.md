## KQ1. What open problems / "what we don't understand" statements do surveys, position papers, talks and conclusion sections make?

### Takeaway
The community's self-declared open problems cluster into five recurring themes: (1) the mechanism of ICL "remains unclear" even in 2024 surveys; (2) solvable toy models (layerwise-linear, linear-attention, orthogonal-skill) are explicitly offered *as a research program* with acknowledged limits — nonlinear activations, softmax attention, nonlinear targets, cross-skill interactions, offline/repeated-data effects; (3) in feature learning, the headline open problem is whether SGD-like algorithms can reach the generative-exponent sample complexity *agnostically* (without knowing the label transformation) and what role the second layer / learning-rate ratio plays; (4) in emergence, whether "quanta"/skills are discrete, whether transitions are noise, and whether emergence timing is intrinsically stochastic; (5) scaling-law theorists now say exponents are largely understood and the frontier has moved to coefficients, feature-learning corrections and the depth/width/context trade-off.

### Cited Findings

**Surveys / position papers**

- Dong et al., "A Survey on In-context Learning" (arXiv 2301.00234; EMNLP 2024). The conclusion says the paper "identifies critical challenges and potential directions for future research"; its limitations section lists "long-context ICL, efficiency and scalability in ICL" as future directions; and it states that "despite preliminary explanations, the underlying working mechanism of ICL remains unclear." — [arXiv 2301.00234](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.00234); [ACL Anthology 2024.emnlp-main.64](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.64/)
- Nam et al., "Position: Solve Layerwise Linear Models First to Understand Neural Dynamical Phenomena (Neural Collapse, Emergence, Lazy/Rich Regime, and Grokking)" (ICML 2025, arXiv 2502.21009). The position: researchers should "prioritize the dynamics of layerwise structure as the key feature over the non-linear activations, until its limits are fully explored." Unifying idea: the "dynamical feedback principle" (each factor's magnitude sets how fast the other factor changes; a shallow linear model lacks this coupling). Acknowledged limits in the snippets: "the dynamics of linear neural networks is challenging to solve in general"; strongly imbalanced layers are a boundary case where "the heavier layer receives negligible gradients, remaining nearly fixed and disrupting nonlinear reinforcement between layers"; the authors "prioritize intuitive derivations over rigorous mathematical proofs"; and they "advocate for further research using solvable models and the dynamical feedback principle." — [arXiv 2502.21009](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.21009); [ICML 2025 poster page](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/poster/40104)
- Bai, Chen, Wang, Xiong, Mei, "Transformers as Statisticians" (NeurIPS 2023, arXiv 2306.04637): the paper "lays out a foundation for the intriguing special case where the learning targets are themselves ICL algorithms"; listed future directions (seen only as a fragment): "ICL procedures beyond in-context algorithm selection; further statistical analyses, e.g. of pretraining." — [Semantic Scholar entry](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Transformers-as-Statisticians:-Provable-In-Context-Bai-Chen/70c3d5ab03a54281be91709b19e3f50a2e4be0e3); [OpenReview PDF](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=vlCG5HKEkI)
- Ahn, Cheng, Daneshmand, Sra (NeurIPS 2023): closing call — "Our results call for future theoretical studies on learning algorithms by training transformers." — [arXiv 2306.00297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00297)
- Zhang, Frei, Bartlett, "Trained Transformers Learn Linear Models In-Context" (JMLR 2024): stated limitation — "Although gradient flow succeeds at finding a global minimum in this setting, the trained transformer is still brittle under mild covariate shifts"; a follow-up by the same group states that "questions about the necessary sample complexity, pretraining task diversity, and context length for successful ICL remain unresolved." — [JMLR paper](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume25/23-1042/23-1042.pdf); [arXiv 2306.09927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09927)

**Scaling-law theory (Pehlevan/Bordelon group and neighbours)**

- Bordelon, Letey, Pehlevan, "Theory of Scaling Laws for In-Context Regression: Depth, Width, Context and Time" (ICLR 2026, arXiv 2510.01098). Limitations section (quoted in snippets): "The primary limitation is that we focus on linear regression tasks solved with linear attention"; "Characterization of more general ICL problems such as nonlinear function approximation and nonlinear attention could provide more insights into realistic in-context function approximation"; "Further, our analysis is focused on online learning in the present work. Key result: "depth only aids ICL performance if context length is limited" for isotropic/fixed-covariance settings, but when covariances vary across contexts "increasing the depth leads to significant improvements in ICL, even at infinite context length." — [arXiv 2510.01098](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.01098); [mlanthology ICLR 2026](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2026/bordelon2026iclr-theory/)

- Bordelon et al., "How Feature Learning Can Improve Neural Scaling Laws" (ICLR 2025 / JSTAT 2025): feature learning "improves the scaling exponent for hard tasks, but not for easy and super-easy tasks." — [Pehlevan lab PDF](https://pehlevan.seas.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum6471/files/2025-03/Bordelon_etal_ICLR_2025_0.pdf)

**Feature learning / information & generative exponent (Lee, Wu, Bruna, Damian, Abbe)**

- Damian, Pillaud-Vivien, Lee, Bruna (2024) define the generative exponent as "the smallest information exponent after arbitrary L2 transformation" of the link; reusing samples lets SGD go beyond the information exponent, "its complexity is governed by the generative-exponent." — [arXiv 2403.05529](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.05529); [Repetita Iuvant, arXiv 2405.15459](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15459)
- Explicit open question (Repetita Iuvant, Arous–Gerbelot–et al.): "when the optimal transformation f is known, a simple SGD algorithm on (z, f(y)) achieves the Generative Exponent lower bound. However, one problem remains: does there exist a class of SGD-like algorithms that can achieve this lower bound agnostically?" — [arXiv 2405.15459](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15459v2)
- Lee, Oko, Suzuki, Wu (NeurIPS 2024): "there exists an 'optimal' label transformation that reduces the information exponent to p*. If SGD can make use of such transformation ... it is natural to conjecture that a sample size of n ≃ d^{p*-1} is sufficient"; also "there exists (non-polynomial) σ* with generative exponent p* > 2 (i.e., label transformations cannot lower the information exponent to 2)." — [arXiv 2406.01581](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.01581)
- "Learning a Single Index Model from Anisotropic Data with vanilla SGD" (arXiv 2503.23642): "achieving the generative exponent in sample complexity by reusing data remains an open question." — [arXiv 2503.23642](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.23642)
- "From Information to Generative Exponent: Learning Rate Induces Phase Transitions in SGD" (arXiv 2510.21020): uses a two-timescale algorithm with different learning rates for first and second layers and shows "the performance of this algorithm also depends critically on the learning rate of the second layer." Third-party summary lists multi-index models and non-Gaussian data as future work. — [arXiv 2510.21020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.21020); [Lacuna summary](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/from-information-to-generative-exponent-learning-rate-induces-phase-transitions/wrk_dd4f485e9e6a4d8d1f6904bd8c8db01d)
- Erdogdu and coauthors call "training both layers simultaneously" an "interesting direction, which is commonly used in practice, yet poorly understood in theory." — [Erdogdu et al. PDF](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~erdogdu/papers/sgd-fl.pdf)
- Abbe, Boix-Adserà, Misiakiewicz (COLT 2023): the "leap" measures how hierarchical a target is; conjectured SGD time complexity d^{max(leap,2)} for fully-connected networks on isotropic data (no transformer version found). — [COLT 2023 PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v195/abbe23a/abbe23a.pdf)
- Oko, Song, Suzuki, Wu (NeurIPS 2024) limitations (only first item retrievable): the in-context sample complexity they derive (r to a power tied to link degree) "matches what polynomial regression or kernel methods require," so the upper bound is not obviously better than classical baselines. — [arXiv 2411.02544](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.02544)
- Talks: Jason Lee, Simons Institute, 19 Feb 2025, "Emergence and scaling laws for SGD learning and Learning Compositional Functions with Transformers" (two-part talk; part 1 on online SGD for a two-layer net with P≫1 orthogonal neurons and power-law second layer a_p=p^{-β}). — [Simons talk page](https://simons.berkeley.edu/talks/jason-lee-princeton-university-2025-02-19). Eshaan Nichani, UT Austin ECE, 4 Feb 2026, "Foundations of language models: scaling and reasoning" (how scaling laws arise from SGD dynamics in shallow nets; conditions under which GD-trained transformers learn ICL and multi-step reasoning). — [UT Austin event](https://ece.utexas.edu/events/foundations-language-models-scaling-and-reasoning); NYU MaD seminar 11 Sep 2025 — [NYU CDS](https://cds.nyu.edu/mc-events/mad-seminar-eshaan-nichani/?mc_id=82); UC Davis STA 290, 6 Nov 2025 — [UC Davis](https://statistics.ucdavis.edu/event/seminar-110625-nichani)

**Emergence / quanta / skills**

- Michaud, Liu, Girit, Tegmark, "The Quantization Model of Neural Scaling" (NeurIPS 2023) — open questions stated by the authors: "it remains to be seen whether scaling totally changes what networks learn, rather than simply adding additional quanta"; sharp per-token transitions "could be due to noise" (multiple seeds needed); rigorous generalization to the "polygenic" case is "an interesting challenge for future work"; "it is unclear how to break up natural language prediction into quanta a priori"; empirical sparse-parity exponents "do not perfectly match" theory; LM evidence is "tentative." — [arXiv 2303.13506](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.13506); [Michaud 2026 blog repost](https://learningmechanics.pub/quanta/)
- Nam, Fonseca, Lee, Mingard, Louis (NeurIPS 2024) solvable multitask-sparse-parity model: skills are orthogonal basis functions with power-law frequencies (exponent α+1); the model "cannot capture the complex non-linear interactions among multiple skills but can express any linear superposition of skills." — [arXiv 2404.17563](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.17563); [NeurIPS 2024 PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/45f7ad60c01f17711ccd8ac2f2fb77e3-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Baherwani, Chen, Qiu, Wilson, Izmailov, "Emergent Capabilities Arise Randomly from Learning Sparse Attention Patterns" (arXiv 2606.25010, June 2026): stated limitations — Pythia experiments "limited to models below 1B parameters," tasks are "relatively elementary," and "whether the findings generalize to more sophisticated capabilities and larger models is an open question"; synthetic tasks use positional patterns and "context-dependent patterns such as induction heads may exhibit qualitatively different properties." Future directions: attention interventions during training, sparsity-biased attention, distillation of attention maps, "synthetic pre-pretraining." — [arXiv 2606.25010](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25010); [alphaXiv overview](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2606.25010)
- Wang, Dang, Lee, Lyu, "The Power of Power Law: Asymmetry Enables Compositional Reasoning" (ICML 2026, arXiv 2604.22951): authors acknowledge "most of the experiment settings in our paper are based on the pretraining setting on algorithmic or synthetic natural language tasks"; "the analysis does not rule out other asymmetric distributions that enable LLMs to acquire composition capabilities," and they "conjecture that the granularity of the asymmetry matters"; exponent trade-off: "the tail skills' learning will be slowed down due to smaller sampling probability, so the α = 1.5 one falls behind in the end." — [arXiv 2604.22951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22951); [ICML 2026 poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/64765)
- Liu, Sun, Li, Lee, Tjuatja, Huang, Neubig, "What do Language Models Learn and When? The Implicit Curriculum Hypothesis" (arXiv 2604.08510): motivation — "Scaling laws on validation loss tell us how much a model improves with additional compute, but not what skills it acquires in which order"; conclusion says results "open several avenues," naming pretraining monitoring (is a model ahead of or behind schedule?) and that task structure "could also inform data mixture design." Consistency drops under relative thresholds (Table 6). — [arXiv 2604.08510](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08510v1); [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.08510)
- Mehta & Gupta, "Scaling Laws and In-Context Learning: A Unified Theoretical Framework" (arXiv 2511.06232; PriGM workshop, EurIPS 2025): claims ICL performance follows power laws in depth, width, context length and data "with exponents determined by task structure," an effective in-forward-pass learning rate Θ(1/√(Ld)), and optimal shape depth ∝ N^{2/3}, width ∝ N^{1/3}; validation is synthetic only. — [arXiv 2511.06232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06232); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=7RhquqRWFG)
- Gu, Xu, Zdeborová, "In-context Learning of Single-index Targets: Comparing Kernel and Feature Learners" (arXiv 2610.01712, 1 Oct 2026): replica-method phase diagrams over pretraining data, task diversity, and context lengths; "qualitatively different context-length scalings for the two learners." Abstract mirror (German-language, alphaXiv) states the fixed-kernel learner needs context length of order d^k to recover a degree-k harmonic component while the feature learner's transition occurs at order d and can use all Hermite components together, but at L=Θ(d) its error can remain positive. — [arXiv 2610.01712](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01712); [alphaXiv (de)](https://www.alphaxiv.org/de/abs/2610.01712)
- Arora & Goyal, "A Theory for Emergence of Complex Skills" (2023): relies on a random bipartite skill–text graph and a Chinchilla-like law ("10x up-scaling leads to factor 2 reduction in excess entropy"); later critique: under this framework "there is no phase transition and conforms to the notion that emergence is slow," and "these existing frameworks explain neither the Chinchilla rule nor the plateau phenomenon"; a density-evolution extension concludes that "deriving simple scaling laws ... is unlikely" for hierarchical skills. — [arXiv 2307.15936](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15936); [arXiv 2410.01243](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01243v1)

### Inferences
- Position papers in this field tend to argue for a *modelling program* (layerwise-linear, linear-attention, orthogonal skills) and the "open problems" they state are the program's own boundary conditions (nonlinearity, interactions, softmax, offline data), rather than lists of unexplained phenomena.
- The feature-learning community has converged on a crisp, formal open problem (agnostic attainment of the generative exponent by SGD-like dynamics, and the role of the second layer / learning-rate ratio), whereas the emergence community's open problems are still partly definitional (what is a quantum/skill; is a transition noise; is emergence stochastic).
- Several 2026 papers (2606.25010, 2606.29858 "Smooth Scaling Laws Hide Stepwise Token Learning", 2604.08510) converge on the question "what is learned when, and is the ordering predictable," which can be read as the successor to the 2023 "mirage vs. real" debate.

## KQ2. For influential papers: what question did they ask, why was it non-obvious, and what kind of answer did they give?

### Takeaway
Across the 17 papers surveyed, the dominant answer types are *mechanism* (ICL ≈ GD / preconditioned GD / induction heads / attention-pattern formation), *phase diagram or threshold* (task diversity, burstiness, context length, depth), *quantitative law* (power-law exponents from information exponent or skill frequencies), *debunking* (metric-induced emergence; non-Bayesian ICL), and *separation* (feature vs kernel learners; softmax vs linear attention). The most-cited questions are strikingly short ("what learning algorithm is ICL?", "what can transformers learn in context?", "are emergent abilities a mirage?") and each is non-obvious because an accepted default (memorization/indexing, Bayesian inference, smooth scaling) predicted the opposite.

NeurIPS 2023 Outstanding Paper. — [arXiv 2304.15004](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.15004) |

| 17 | He, Doshi, Das, Gromov 2024 (Learning to grok) | When does a transformer trained on a finite set of modular-arithmetic tasks generalize to *unseen* tasks (ICL + skill composition)? | Grokking was single-task; OOD task generalization unexpected. | Phase diagram: shift from ID to OOD generalization as the number of pre-training tasks grows; "the smallest model capable of out-of-distribution generalization requires two transformer blocks"; for deeper models "the out-of-distribution generalization phase is transient, necessitating early stopping"; multi-task loss "would stagnate at a plateau indefinitely." NeurIPS 2024 oral. — [arXiv 2406.02550](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02550); [NeurIPS oral](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/oral/97968) |

Additional framing papers:
- Mahankali, Hashimoto, Ma (ICLR 2024): the global minimizer of pretraining loss for one layer of linear self-attention "implements a single step of GD on a least-squares linear regression objective," even when responses come from nonlinear functions (optimality-type answer). — [arXiv 2307.03576](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.03576)
- Ahn, Cheng, Daneshmand, Sra (NeurIPS 2023): one-layer global minimum "implements a single iteration of preconditioned gradient descent"; the preconditioner "adapts to the input distribution [and] to the variance induced by data inadequacy"; L-layer critical points implement L steps. — [arXiv 2306.00297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00297)
- Hahn & Goyal (2023): information-theoretic bound showing ICL "arise[s] from generic next-token prediction when the pretraining distribution has sufficient amounts of compositional structure"; second bound explains why writing out intermediate steps helps. — [arXiv 2303.07971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07971)
- Baherwani et al. 2026: "downstream capabilities arise abruptly due to the intrinsic difficulty of learning sparse attention patterns"; "emergent capabilities arise stochastically throughout training, with larger models acquiring them earlier on average"; patching learned attention maps into earlier checkpoints recovers most performance (mechanism + randomness claim). — [arXiv 2606.25010](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25010v1)
- Wurgaft et al., "In-Context Learning Strategies Emerge Rationally" (2025): a hierarchical Bayesian model assuming "simplicity bias and scaling laws as computational constraints" that "almost perfectly predicts Transformer next-token predictions without access to the weights"; training involves "a fundamental trade-off between the loss and complexity of potential solutions." — [arXiv 2506.17859](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17859v2)

### Inferences
- Non-obviousness is almost always generated by a *competing default explanation*: indexing/memorization (Garg), Bayesian inference (Raventós), kernel/fixed-feature ICL (Oko; Nishikawa), smooth scaling (Michaud; Ren), fundamental emergence (Schaeffer), persistence (Singh). A compelling question in this field thus states both the phenomenon and the default it would overturn.
- The answer type has drifted over time: 2022–23 mechanism and debunking; 2023–24 thresholds/phase diagrams; 2024–26 quantitative exponents and separations (feature vs kernel; softmax vs linear), plus "ordering/predictability" claims.

## KQ3. What does the community explicitly say would count as progress?

### Takeaway
Explicit "progress criteria" statements are rarer than open-problem lists, but several are on record: for ICL theory, go beyond linear regression with linear attention to nonlinear targets and nonlinear (softmax) attention, and account for offline/repeated data and large learning rates; for feature learning, exhibit an SGD-like algorithm attaining the generative-exponent rate agnostically; for emergence, show whether emergence timing is intrinsically stochastic and whether skill-acquisition order is predictable enough to *monitor pretraining*; for scaling laws, predict compute-optimal shape (depth/width/context) and coefficients, not just exponents.

### Cited Findings
- ICL theory progress = realism in task and attention: "Characterization of more general ICL problems such as nonlinear function approximation and nonlinear attention could provide more insights into realistic in-context function approximation" (Bordelon–Letey–Pehlevan). — [arXiv 2510.01098](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.01098)
- Progress on linear-vs-softmax: von Oswald et al. "call for more study of linear versus softmax attention on real-world tasks"; a NeurIPS 2024 paper notes "a noticeable gap remains between linear attention and softmax attention" and that earlier analyses "assume linear attention and mostly study linear regression, which limits how far they explain in-context learning in general." — [ICML 2023 PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/von-oswald23a/von-oswald23a.pdf); [NeurIPS 2024 PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/01a8d63f9cb6dcbaa3092ccddd2075ac-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Progress in feature learning = agnostic generative-exponent attainment: "does there exist a class of SGD-like algorithms that can achieve this lower bound agnostically?" — [arXiv 2405.15459](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15459v2); and conjecture "n ≃ d^{p*-1} is sufficient" if SGD can use the optimal label transformation. — [arXiv 2406.01581](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.01581)
- Progress in understanding transformers via training (not construction): "Our results call for future theoretical studies on learning algorithms by training transformers" (Ahn et al.). — [arXiv 2306.00297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00297)
- Progress on emergence = predictive monitoring: the implicit-curriculum paper proposes that if orderings are stable, the task suite could show "whether a model is developing capabilities ahead of or behind schedule," and task structure "could also inform data mixture design." — [arXiv 2604.08510](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08510v1)
- Progress on emergence = internals, not curves: "Whether scaling truly has the effect we described will have to be investigated in future studies of the internals of neural networks" (Michaud et al.). — [arXiv 2303.13506](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.13506)
- Progress on the mirage debate = prospective metrics: critics note smooth metrics were "often identified after the fact," and "the metric of primary interest may be fundamentally discontinuous" in policy-relevant settings; a 2025 review calls emergence "a contested interpretation of evaluation curves rather than a settled fact." — [Predicting Emergent Capabilities by Finetuning, arXiv 2411.16035](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.16035); [Windows on Theory blog](https://windowsontheory.org/2023/12/22/emergent-abilities-and-grokking-fundamental-mirage-or-both/)
- Progress on scaling laws = shape and coefficients: Bordelon et al. present a model that "depends on both width and depth of a transformer and predicts optimal transformer shapes as a function of compute"; 2606.25008's title: "If Exponents Are Fixed, Time to Understand Coefficients." — [arXiv 2510.01098](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.01098); [arXiv 2606.25008](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.25008)
- Progress on emergence via attention = training interventions: Baherwani et al. propose "attention interventions during training, attention mechanisms with an inductive bias towards sparsity, and distillation of attention maps from stronger models," plus "synthetic pre-pretraining." — [arXiv 2606.25010](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25010)
- Progress on compositional learning via data: Wang–Dang–Lee–Lyu "hope future work will improve real-world skill composition" (e.g., re-weighting real data to power laws, synthesizing data for missing skills — third-party summary). — [arXiv 2604.22951](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.22951); [Lacuna summary](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/the-power-of-power-law-asymmetry-enables-compositional-reasoning/wrk_a238713affe0b69e3e9658721854420f)
- Progress on layerwise-linear program: Nam et al. ask the field to study layerwise structure "until its limits are fully explored." — [arXiv 2502.21009](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.21009)

### Inferences
- The phrases the brief suggested ("a theory that predicts when ICL emerges in a real transformer", "linking information exponent results to transformers", "why does ICL need long context", "when is composition free") were *not* found verbatim; the closest on-record equivalents are: Bordelon et al.'s "nonlinear function approximation and nonlinear attention"; Oko–Song–Suzuki–Wu / Nishikawa linking pretraining sample complexity to the information exponent in a transformer; Gu–Xu–Zdeborová's "qualitatively different context-length scalings"; and Okawa / Wang–Dang–Lee–Lyu on when composition is learnable at all.

### Gaps
- No single document found that lists "what would count as progress" for ICL theory as a community statement (e.g., a 2025–26 workshop report). The ICML 2024 ICL workshop stated the aim to "map out open problems," but its outcome document was not located.

## KQ4. Explicit flags about five specific unresolved issues

### Takeaway
(i) Pretraining-time context length is only beginning to be theorized (Lu et al.; Bordelon et al.; Gu–Xu–Zdeborová; Wu et al. 2310.08391 show dimension-free context dependence once task count is large) and no paper found poses it as a feature-learning question per se; (ii) the second-layer/readout's effect on which exponent governs learning is flagged via the learning-rate-ratio result and the "training both layers simultaneously ... poorly understood" remark; (iii) "composition for free" is contested — Arora–Goyal predict free recombination, while Okawa (multiplicative delays), He et al. (depth ≥2, transience), and Wang–Dang–Lee–Lyu (uniform sampling can make state tracking unlearnable) show it is conditional; (iv) the toy-to-softmax gap is explicitly flagged by von Oswald, Bordelon et al., and 2024–25 softmax analyses; (v) the IWL-vs-ICL (task-mean vs task-variance) trade-off is framed as a data-distribution trade-off (Chan; Reddy; Azizian–Hasan; Wurgaft), not as a solved mechanism.

### Cited Findings

**(i) Context length in pretraining-time feature learning**
- Wu et al., "How Many Pretraining Tasks Are Needed for In-Context Learning of Linear Regression?": once enough pretraining tasks exist, "the dependence on the prompt context length becomes dimension-free"; also "when the context length in inference significantly differs from that in pretraining, the pretrained single-layer linear attention model might be suboptimal." — [arXiv 2310.08391](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.08391)
- Bordelon–Letey–Pehlevan: for isotropic/fixed-covariance settings "there is no benefit to increasing depth L beyond L = 1 in the large context α → ∞ limit"; the scaling limit takes "data dimension, context length, and residual stream width to grow together." — [arXiv 2510.01098](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.01098)
- Gu–Xu–Zdeborová retain "training and inference context lengths" as separate replica order parameters and report different context-length scalings for kernel vs feature learners (d^k vs d). — [arXiv 2610.01712](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01712)
- Baherwani et al.: "varying the context length or attention pattern sparsity dictates whether a model solves a task completely or makes no progress at all"; "the difficulty of learning an attention pattern depends on context length and pattern sparsity." — [arXiv 2606.25010](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25010v1)
- "Transformers as Cross-Task Learners" (2026): GD learns a shared low-dimensional subspace of single-index tasks so in-context sample complexity "depend[s] on intrinsic rather than ambient task dimension." — [arXiv 2609.29060](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29060v1)

**(ii) Readout / second layer and which exponent governs learning**
- 2510.21020: two-timescale training with distinct first/second-layer learning rates; "the performance of this algorithm also depends critically on the learning rate of the second layer"; title claims learning rate induces phase transitions between information- and generative-exponent regimes. — [arXiv 2510.21020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.21020)
- Lee–Oko–Suzuki–Wu: Phase I recovers direction, then "training the second-layer parameters in Phase II is sufficient for the NN model to achieve small generalization error." — [NeurIPS 2024 PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/6bd5fca2074dcd9ede9de50f71f7ec28-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Ren–Nichani–Wu–Lee allow "a diverging condition number in the second layer," covering power-law a_p ∝ p^{-β}, and derive how second-layer structure sets the scaling exponents. — [arXiv 2504.19983](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19983v2)
- Erdogdu et al.: "training both layers simultaneously ... commonly used in practice, yet poorly understood in theory." — [Erdogdu PDF](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~erdogdu/papers/sgd-fl.pdf)
- Gu–Xu–Zdeborová's "feature learner" is defined by placing a *learned nonlinear readout after attention*; the kernel learner places a fixed map before attention; the paper's phase diagrams are precisely about which placement wins. — [arXiv 2610.01712](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01712)

**(iii) When compositional skills emerge "for free"**
- Arora–Goyal predict LLMs "combine skills at test time despite never having seen the same combination during training." — [ICLR 2024 invited talk](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2024/23438)
- Okawa et al.: composition emerges multiplicatively; OOD compositions require "considerably more optimization steps"; "learning and composing capabilities is difficult under correlations." — [arXiv 2310.09336](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09336)
- He et al.: OOD task generalization needs ≥2 blocks and is transient in deeper models. — [arXiv 2406.02550](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02550)
- Wang–Dang–Lee–Lyu: switching skill sampling from uniform to power law "turns an unlearnable task (e.g. state tracking) into a learnable one." — [arXiv 2604.22951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22951)
- Liu et al.: "composite tasks most often emerge after their component tasks"; a third-party summary notes composites carry structure components don't capture ("compositional bottleneck"). — [arXiv 2604.08510](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08510v1); [arxiviq summary](https://arxiviq.substack.com/p/what-do-language-models-learn-and)
- Nam et al. 2024 solvable model "cannot capture the complex non-linear interactions among multiple skills." — [arXiv 2404.17563](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.17563)

**(iv) Gap between toy linear-attention(+MLP) models and softmax transformers**
- von Oswald et al.: linear self-attention construction; softmax "weaker"; calls for study of linear vs softmax on real tasks. — [ICML 2023 PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/von-oswald23a/von-oswald23a.pdf)
- Bordelon–Letey–Pehlevan: "primary limitation is that we focus on linear regression tasks solved with linear attention." — [arXiv 2510.01098](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.01098)
- Zhang–Frei–Bartlett: single linear SA layer "brittle under mild covariate shifts," whereas "large, nonlinear transformer architectures [were] more robust." — [JMLR](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume25/23-1042/23-1042.pdf)
- "Softmax ≥ Linear" (2025): softmax transformers do GD-like learning "on functionals in a kernel feature space with a context-adaptive learning rate." — [arXiv 2510.10425](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10425)
- Nishikawa et al.: softmax's exponential is what enables inference-time feature learning (so the gap is not just quantitative). — [ICML 2025](https://mlanthology.org/icml/2025/nishikawa2025icml-nonlinear/)
- "Softmax as Linear Attention in the Large-Prompt Regime" (2025) restricted to one layer; in deep models "the output of one layer becomes the non-Gaussian input of the next" (third-party summary). — [arXiv 2512.11784](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11784)
- Lu et al. claim their linear-attention insights "transfer to full Transformer models with softmax self-attention." — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2502599122)
- "How Data Mixing Shapes In-Context Learning: Asymptotic Equivalence for Transformers with MLPs" (2025) addresses the +MLP case. — [arXiv 2510.25753](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25753)

**(v) Task-mean vs task-variance (in-weights vs in-context)**
- Chan et al.: initial experiments showed ICL "traded off against" IWL; Zipfian class skew allows both. — [arXiv 2205.05055](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05055)
- Reddy (ICLR 2024): "burstiness, large dictionaries, and skewed rank-frequency distributions, control the trade-off or simultaneous appearance of these two forms of learning." — [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=aN4Jf6Cx69)
- Singh et al.: ICL replaced by IWL over training; Nguyen & Reddy attribute this to "different relative learning rates of ICL and IWL"; "Strategy Coopetition" (2025) addresses the same. — [arXiv 2311.08360](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.08360); [arXiv 2503.05631](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.05631)
- Azizian & Hasan, "How Does the Pretraining Distribution Shape In-Context Learning? A Fundamental Trade-Off" (ICML 2026): "heavy-tailed pretraining distributions facilitate robust task selection under distribution shifts but are detrimental to generalization, especially in low-data regimes"; proven for an idealized Bayes-optimal learner. — [arXiv 2510.01163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01163)
- Wurgaft et al.: within-class variance σ as the noise parameter in a finite task mixture; loss-vs-complexity trade-off. — [arXiv 2506.17859](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17859v2)
- "Learning Linear Regression with Low-Rank Tasks in-Context": "a sharp phase transition governed by task structure," a "trade-off between specialization and robustness under distributional shifts." — [arXiv 2510.04548](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.04548)
- Cognitive-science framing: PNAS 2025 "natural trade-off: when in-context learning succeeds, fewer errors accumulate, so there are fewer weight updates and less in-weights learning"; Nature Human Behaviour 2025: "redundancy and diversity trade off in driving in-weights and in-context learning." — [PNAS 2025](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2510270122); [Nature Human Behaviour](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02359-3)

### Inferences
- Issue (i) is the least explicitly theorized: the retrieved papers treat context length as a resource in the *ICL* scaling limit, not as a driver of *pretraining-time feature learning*; Gu–Xu–Zdeborová (Oct 2026) is the first found to carry training- and inference-context lengths as separate order parameters for nonlinear targets.
- Issue (ii) now has a concrete mechanism candidate (second-layer learning-rate ratio selects the information vs generative exponent regime), but it is stated for MLPs, not transformers.
- Issue (iii)'s literature splits by whether composition is *representational* (Arora–Goyal: free) or *dynamical* (Okawa, He, Wang et al.: conditional on data distribution, depth, and training time).

### Gaps
- No paper found that uses the literal terms "task mean" and "task variance" for IWL vs ICL; the framing in this brief appears to be the requester's, and the closest formal analogues are listed above.
- No source found explicitly posing "how does pretraining context length shape feature learning (e.g., which exponent governs pretraining sample complexity)?" as an open problem.
- Nguyen & Reddy and "Strategy Coopetition" content not retrieved; cited via Singh et al. search summaries only.
