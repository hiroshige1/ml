## KQ1. Typical ICML/NeurIPS/ICLR workshop CFP requirements and timelines; ICML 2026 workshop deadlines; what is known about ICML 2027

### Takeaway
Conference-level rules fix only the skeleton (one-day in-person workshops, OpenReview submission, a "suggested" contribution deadline and a *mandatory* universal notification date); page limits, archival status and dual-submission rules are set per workshop, and nearly all theory workshops in this space run non-archival. For ICML 2026 the suggested workshop-paper deadline was 24 April 2026 and the universal notification deadline was 14–15 May 2026; for ICML 2027 the only official fact is "South America," with no city, dates or CFP published yet.

## KQ2. What workshop organisers/reviewers say they want: criteria (clarity of question, correctness, novelty relative to recent arXiv, significance of toy model, honest limitations), preliminary vs complete, negative results, position papers

### Takeaway
No HiLD/M3L reviewer form was retrievable, but the general reviewing literature and the theory-workshop scope statements converge: correctness first, a clearly stated question, honest scope, and (for theory) a stated connection to practice; workshops explicitly position themselves as venues for preliminary, hypothesis-driven and exploratory work, and some workshops specifically solicit negative results and position papers.

### Cited Findings
- JMLR reviewer guide (widely used as a template): check that all claims are "clearly articulated and supported either by empirical experiments or theoretical analyses"; theory papers "should also discuss their practical utility"; descriptions should be detailed enough for replication. — [JMLR reviewer guide](https://www.jmlr.org/beta/reviewer-guide.html)
- ICML review form asks reviewers to restate contributions in their own words and to separate major from minor concerns. — [ICML 2022 Review Form](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2022/ReviewForm)
- ICLR 2025 "I Can't Believe It's Not Better" workshop: submissions "evaluated based on rigor, novelty, clarity, reproducibility, and the depth of their insights"; the workshop series explicitly solicits negative/unexpected results. — [ICBINB ICLR 2025 CFP](https://lists.cnsorg.org/hyperkitty/list/comp-neuro@lists.cnsorg.org/thread/VVETGKHYB5WTIZUST3INQZEG7ZLIVZ2A/)
- The "Insights from Negative Results" workshop series (NLP) invites "both practical and theoretical unexpected or negative results" and requires that a failure demonstration be "accompanied by some explanation/hypothesis"; it notes that negative results are hard to publish and that a 2010 ACL negative-results track received too few submissions. — [Insights workshop, ACL portal](https://www.aclweb.org/portal/node/5984); [2023 proceedings front matter](https://aclanthology.org/2023.insights-1.pdf)
- Sci4DL (ICLR 2026) / SciForDL (NeurIPS 2024) stated aim: "promote a complementary approach that is centered on the use of the scientific method, which forms hypotheses and designs controlled experiments to test them," contrasting with the mathematical approach that "aims to prove rigorous theorems about optimization or generalization errors." — [SciForDL NeurIPS 2024 page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/workshop/84741); [Sci4DL ICLR 2026 page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/workshop/10000783)
- HiLD 2024 topics explicitly include "emergence of interpretable behaviors (e.g., circuit mechanisms) and capabilities (e.g., compositionality and reasoning)", "scaling laws related to internal structures and functional differences", "competition among structures and heuristics, such as simplicity bias", and tools from "stochastic differential equations, high-dimensional probability, random matrix theory." — [Pehlevan lab announcement of 2nd HiLD](https://pehlevan.seas.harvard.edu/news/2nd-workshop-high-dimensional-learning-dynamics-hild-emergence-structure-and-reasoning)
- HiLD 2026 framing: "the field still lacks a unified mathematical framework linking optimizer dynamics, architecture choice, and data structure to the power-law exponents seen in large-scale training"; the aim is "a rigorous science of scaling" bringing together "theoreticians and practitioners who build and train frontier models." — [HiLD 2026 ICML page](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/workshop/54077)
- Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop (ICML 2026) frames its program around "the arising debate between pragmatic and ambitious approaches in the field," and reports its NeurIPS 2025 edition drew over 600 attendees. — [Mech Interp Workshop ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/workshop/54071)
- Reviewer-variability evidence: Eric Jang's reviewing post argues NeurIPS acceptance is "something of a weighted dice roll" because reviewers "each favor different things." — [evjang.com, 2020](https://blog.evjang.com/2020/09/reviewing.html)
- Colin Raffel's reviewing-criteria checklist is explicitly scoped to main venues and says the same rules "may not apply to … different types of venues like journals and workshops." — [colinraffel.com](https://colinraffel.com/blog/reviewing-criteria.html)
- Reddit anecdote (low-weight): a workshop reviewer given no guidelines got advice ranging from "relevance first" to "novelty focus" and was told to ask the chairs. — [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1ticoy5/snapshots/2026-05-20T23%3A40%3A07.432Z)
- Status of workshop papers: a graduate-school guide describes the pipeline "short workshop papers → conference papers → journal articles" and notes it is "usually okay to publish the same or substantially similar papers in multiple workshops." — [U. Alabama graduate guide](https://graduate.ua.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/How-to-Succeed-in-Graduate-School_Part2.pdf); one commenter estimated a workshop paper as "1/3–1/2 as impressive as a conference paper" (opinion). — [Manifund comment](https://dev.manifund.org/NeelNanda)
- A 2025 position paper argues ML conferences should add a "Refutations and Critiques" track, listing famous near-rejections (Knowledge Distillation rejected at NeurIPS 2014; Adam initially rejected at ICLR 2015). — [arXiv 2506.19882](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.19882)

### Inferences
- For a theory-flavoured HiLD/M3L/Sci4DL submission the de-facto rubric is: (i) a crisp question tied to a named empirical phenomenon (emergence, scaling exponent, grokking, induction heads); (ii) a model simple enough to solve but with the mechanism mapped to the phenomenon; (iii) correctness and a clear statement of assumptions; (iv) at least one prediction checked outside the solvable model. This is inferred from the scope statements plus the JMLR "practical utility" criterion; no theory-workshop reviewer form was found.
- Negative results and position papers are welcome at some workshops (ICBINB, Insights) but theory workshops' CFPs found here do not explicitly solicit them; Sci4DL's "hypothesis + controlled experiment" framing is the closest fit for a theory-informed negative result.

### Gaps
- No HiLD, M3L, TF2M or ICL-workshop reviewer form or review criteria text was retrievable (OpenReview/Google Sites blocked).
- No organiser blog post or retrospective explicitly listing "what we wanted" for a theory workshop was found; the criteria above are reconstructed from scope statements and general reviewing guides.

## KQ3. Concrete examples of workshop papers in ICL theory / grokking / induction heads / scaling / emergence that became influential or main-conference papers, with the "one sharp claim"

### Takeaway
Six verified workshop-first papers were found (grokking, two scaling/grokking papers from the NeurIPS 2022 ML Safety workshop, emergence-as-mirage, a solvable emergence model from HiLD 2024, and statistical induction heads from SciForDL 2024). Each makes one sharp, falsifiable claim about a named empirical puzzle, often in a deliberately minimal setting. Several of the most-cited ICL-theory papers (von Oswald, Akyürek, Raventós) did *not* pass through a workshop first.

### Cited Findings
1. **Grokking** — Power, Burda, Edwards, Babuschkin, Misra (OpenAI). Workshop: "1st Mathematical Reasoning in General Artificial Intelligence Workshop, ICLR 2021"; arXiv version Jan 2022 (2201.02177). Sharp claim: on small algorithmic datasets, networks jump from chance to perfect generalization long after overfitting, and smaller datasets require ever more optimization to generalize. — [Gwern-hosted workshop PDF](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/ai/nn/fully-connected/2021-power.pdf); [arXiv 2201.02177](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.02177)
2. **Are Emergent Abilities of LLMs a Mirage?** — Schaeffer, Miranda, Koyejo. Workshop poster at ICML 2023 "Challenges in Deployable Generative AI"; arXiv v1 28 Apr 2023; then NeurIPS 2023 main track, oral / award nomination. Sharp claim: apparent emergence is an artifact of the researcher's choice of (discontinuous) metric, not of a change in model behavior with scale. — [ICML 2023 workshop listing](https://icml.cc/virtual/2023/28956); [NeurIPS 2023 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72117); [Stanford AI Lab NeurIPS 2023 roundup](https://ai.stanford.edu/blog/neruips-2023)
3. **Broken Neural Scaling Laws** — Caballero, Gupta, Rish, Krueger. NeurIPS 2022 Workshop on ML Safety (MLSW); later ICLR 2023 (the ICLR acceptance is from my background knowledge; the search confirmed only the workshop record). Sharp claim: a "smoothly broken power law" functional form fits and extrapolates scaling behaviour including non-monotonic transitions (double descent) and delayed sharp inflections (arithmetic). — [ML Anthology record](https://mlanthology.org/neuripsw/2022/caballero2022neuripsw-broken)
4. **Unifying Grokking and Double Descent** — Davies, Langosco, Krueger. NeurIPS 2022 MLSW. Sharp claim: grokking and double descent are instances of the same dynamics ("pattern learning speeds"); predicts and demonstrates *model-wise* grokking. — [ML Anthology record](https://mlanthology.org/neuripsw/2022/davies2022neuripsw-unifying)
5. **An Exactly Solvable Model for Emergence and Scaling Laws** — Nam et al. ICML 2024 HiLD workshop → NeurIPS 2024 main track (retitled "...in the multitask sparse parity problem"; arXiv v3 "Accepted at NeurIPS 2024"). Sharp claim: representing each skill as a basis function and solving a multilinear model in the skill basis gives closed-form expressions for when skills emerge and for loss scaling laws; validated on a two-layer net trained on multitask sparse parity with power-law task frequencies. — [ML Anthology HiLD record](https://mlanthology.org/icmlw/2024/nam2024icmlw-exactly); [ICML 2024 workshop entry](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/35750); [arXiv 2404.17563v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17563v3); [NeurIPS 2024 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/94372)
6. **The Evolution of Statistical Induction Heads: In-Context Learning Markov Chains** — Edelman, Tsilivis, Goel, Edelman, Malach. NeurIPS 2024 workshop "Scientific Methods for Understanding Neural Networks" (SciForDL) and NeurIPS 2024 main-track poster (11 Dec 2024). Sharp claim: on a Markov-chain ICL task, training passes through a near-uniform phase, a unigram shortcut, then a fast jump to the bigram (induction-head) solution; the simpler shortcut *slows* formation of the optimal solution. — [ML Anthology SciForDL record](https://mlanthology.org/neuripsw/2024/edelman2024neuripsw-evolution); [NeurIPS 2024 poster](https://nips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/93487); [arXiv 2402.11004](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.11004)

Counter-examples (influential ICL-theory papers with *no* workshop stage found):
- **Transformers Learn In-Context by Gradient Descent** (von Oswald et al.): arXiv 15 Dec 2022 → ICML 2023 (PMLR 202). DBLP lists only arXiv and ICML; no workshop version. — [DBLP](https://dblp.dagstuhl.de/pid/242/8029.html); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/von-oswald23a.html)
- **What Learning Algorithm is In-Context Learning? Investigations with Linear Models** (Akyürek et al.): ICLR 2023 oral/top-5%; no workshop version found. Sharp claim: transformers trained on linear regression implicitly implement standard learning algorithms (evidence from constructions, probes, behavioural match). — [ICLR 2023 oral](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/oral/12597)
- **Pretraining task diversity and the emergence of non-Bayesian ICL for regression** (Raventós et al.): arXiv June 2023; sharp claim: a task-diversity threshold below which the transformer behaves as a Bayesian estimator with the pretraining prior and above which it matches ridge regression. No workshop version found. — [arXiv 2306.15063](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.15063v1)
- **Grokking as the Transition from Lazy to Rich Training Dynamics** (Kumar, Bordelon, Gershman, Pehlevan): arXiv Oct 2023 → ICLR 2024; no HiLD record found, though the authors spoke at HiLD 2024. — [ICLR 2024 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2024/poster/17515)
- **A Dynamical Model of Neural Scaling Laws** (Bordelon, Atanasov, Pehlevan) ICML 2024 and **4+3 Phases of Compute-Optimal Neural Scaling Laws** (Paquette, Paquette, Xiao, Pennington) NeurIPS 2024: both main-track; no workshop versions found. — [PMLR Bordelon](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/bordelon24a.html); [NeurIPS 2024 Paquette](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/1dccfc3ee01871d05e33457c61037d59-Abstract.html)

### Inferences
- Common structure of the successful workshop-first papers: name an empirical puzzle already visible in the community (grokking, emergence, broken scaling curves, induction heads), reduce it to a minimal synthetic task (modular arithmetic, sparse parity, Markov chains), and state one mechanism that yields a quantitative or qualitative prediction later checked beyond the toy (e.g., model-wise grokking; metric-dependence of emergence; stage-wise induction-head formation).
- The workshop → main-track turnaround for these examples was 4–8 months (HiLD July 2024 → NeurIPS Dec 2024; ICML workshop July 2023 → NeurIPS Dec 2023; ICLR workshop May 2021 → arXiv Jan 2022 / wide influence), consistent with non-archival status permitting resubmission.
- The strongest ICL-as-GD-type results tended to go straight to main venues; workshop venues were most useful for *phenomenological* or *solvable-model* contributions and for critiques/reframings ("mirage").

- Could not verify the ICLR 2023 acceptance of "Broken Neural Scaling Laws" from search results (only the workshop record surfaced).

- Citation counts / "influence" metrics were not retrievable in this environment.

## KQ4. When is a toy model convincing vs dismissed as "too toy"? Why do "known result in new setting" theory papers get lukewarm reviews, and how did authors reframe?

### Takeaway
Direct reviewer language ("too toy") was not found in retrievable sources; what was found is (a) NeurIPS meta-reviews showing that unrealistic assumptions and toy experiments are forgiven when the paper either proves the assumption is necessary, delivers a genuinely new theoretical object, or targets an important recognised problem, and (b) philosophy-of-science and interpretability writing arguing a toy model earns trust when it reproduces a real phenomenon, isolates a testable mechanism, and transfers to scale.

### Cited Findings
- NeurIPS 2019 meta-review: "The main reservation expressed by the reviewers was that the assumptions of the theory are unrealistic... they showed (via a computational hardness result) that some kind of assumption is necessary for a solution to be tractable at all. So, since the resulting method seems reasonable and exhibits good empirical performance, the reviewers and I were mostly willing to look past this assumption." — [NeurIPS 2019 meta-review](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/2019/file/9c19a2aa1d84e04b0bd4bc888792bd1e-MetaReview.html)
- NeurIPS 2020 meta-review (tensor decomposition): "Some reviewers are worried about the lack of numerical experiments and the weak connection to practical algorithms, but this is acceptable for papers with solid theoretical contribution at NeurIPS." — [NeurIPS 2020 meta-review](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2020/file/f9d3a954de63277730a1c66d8b38dee3-MetaReview.html)
- NeurIPS 2020 meta-review (two-layer early dynamics): reviewers objected results were "limited to one hidden layer" and the data assumption "somewhat restrictive"; rebuttal argued "many interesting deep learning theory papers focus on two-layer networks and that this case is already challenging"; accepted. — [NeurIPS 2020 meta-review](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/c6dfc6b7c601ac2978357b7a81e2d7ae-MetaReview.html)
- NeurIPS 2019 meta-review accepted a paper "despite the toy-ish nature of the experiments" because it addressed an important problem, asking for clearer explanation in camera-ready. — [NeurIPS 2019 meta-review](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/2019/file/df0e09d6f25a15a815563df9827f48fa-MetaReview.html)
- John Langford (hunch.net): a learning-theory paper with "the highest average review of any learning theory paper at NIPS" was rejected; later accepted at COLT — illustrating venue-fit mismatch for theory. — [hunch.net](https://hunch.net/?paged=49)
- Philosophy of science (Nguyen / Sullivan): under the "interpretive view," a toy model licenses inferences not by resembling its target but by having an interpretative function mapping model-facts to claims about the target; Sullivan argues ML models used in science function as highly idealized toy models and proposes an "idealization failure hypothesis" for when they fail to yield understanding. — [Cambridge: "Do ML models represent their targets?"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/95A341566AF62EDDD5FAC2535DE59EC1/S0031824823001514a.pdf/do-machine-learning-models-represent-their-targets.pdf); [UCL Discovery: toy surrogate models](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10185677/)
- ERC project framing of the core worry: "since toy models are so divorced from the real world, how do we know they are not leading us astray?" — [CORDIS project 101165535](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101165535)
- Bowers & Love critique: the toy-model approach "risks incoherency and neglects the most basic test; can the model perform its intended task." — [via Cambridge paper above](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/95A341566AF62EDDD5FAC2535DE59EC1/S0031824823001514a.pdf/do-machine-learning-models-represent-their-targets.pdf)
- Interpretability community framing: the "universality hypothesis is the key bet: if it's true, insights from small, reverse-engineered 'toy models' could apply to massive models. If not, our entire approach of using toy models might be flawed." — [alphaXiv overview of Toy Models of Superposition](https://alphaxiv.org/audio/2302.03025v2)
- Opinion (blog comment): "the toy models around superposition have arguably been more productive than all of deep learning theory," because derived techniques (SAE variants) are used in production at a frontier lab. — [GreaterWrong comment](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/2WkeiYFT5p3ph8Pkf/quick-paper-review-there-will-be-a-scientific-theory-of-deep/comment/ZiuAvhhrNFAq22bpm)
- Seminar abstract (Loureiro, 2021): many "exotic" behaviours of deep networks "have shown to hold in models as simple as high-dimensional linear regression" — the standard justification for solvable high-dimensional toy models. — [ICTP seminar announcement](https://lists.ictp.it/pipermail/science-ts/2021-June/006786.html)
- Advice on toy experiments for empirical ideas: "Toy examples are useful to gain conceptual clarity. However, if your idea is primarily empirical ... spend basically zero time running toy experiments." — [GreaterWrong comment on "Touch reality as soon as possible"](https://greaterwrong.com/posts/fqryrxnvpSr5w2dDJ/touch-reality-as-soon-as-possible-when-doing-machine/comment/GE5BXoAhmgyGHKHSj)
- Example of a successful reframing (from KQ3 evidence): Nam et al.'s HiLD version "An exactly solvable model for emergence and scaling laws" was retitled for NeurIPS 2024 as "...in the multitask sparse parity problem," narrowing the claim to the concrete setting where it is proven. — [arXiv 2404.17563v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17563v3)
- Example of reframing-by-mechanism (from KQ3): Edelman et al. attach the generic "stage-wise learning" story to a specific, checkable mechanism (unigram shortcut delays bigram induction head) in a minimal Markov-chain task. — [arXiv 2402.11004](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.11004)

### Inferences
- Synthesising the meta-reviews and philosophy sources, a toy/solvable model is accepted as convincing when at least one of these holds: (1) it reproduces a *named* empirical puzzle (grokking, emergence, broken power law); (2) it produces a quantitative prediction (exponent, threshold, phase boundary) checked on a less-toy system; (3) it gives a separation or necessity result (e.g., hardness showing the assumption is unavoidable); (4) it isolates a mechanism others can test (universality bet). It is dismissed when the claim about the real system is broader than the toy supports, or when it only re-derives a known result with no new prediction.
- For "known result in a new setting," the retitling of Nam et al. and the "necessity of assumption" meta-review suggest two effective reframings: (a) narrow the title/claim to exactly what is proved and foreground the new *object* (closed form, phase diagram) rather than the setting; (b) show the setting is forced (hardness/necessity) or that it yields a prediction the old setting could not.
