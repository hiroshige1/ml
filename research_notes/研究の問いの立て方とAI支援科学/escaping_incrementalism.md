## Key Question 1: What do science-of-science studies and philosophy of science say about incremental vs. disruptive work?

### Takeaway
Bibliometric studies agree that (i) most science is highly conventional, (ii) truly novel/atypical work is rarer, riskier, slower to be recognised, but disproportionately produces the highest-impact papers, and (iii) impact is maximised not by pure novelty but by "mostly conventional + a tail of atypical combinations". Kuhn and Lakatos supply the two classic diagnostics: an *anomaly* that resists the paradigm's puzzle-solving (Kuhn), and a programme that only re-interprets data ad hoc without producing corroborated novel predictions (Lakatos' "degenerating" programme).

### Cited Findings

**Declining disruptiveness (and its critique)**
- Park, Leahey & Funk (Nature 2023) analysed ~45 million papers and 3.9 million patents over six decades using the CD index (range -1 to +1; a paper is "disruptive" if later work cites it instead of its predecessors). They report that papers and patents "are increasingly less likely to break with the past in ways that push science and technology in new directions", across fields and across several citation- and text-based metrics; the decline is "linked to a narrowing in how previous knowledge is used". — [UMN Experts record of Park et al.](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/papers-and-patents-are-becoming-less-disruptive-over-time/); [Technology Networks summary](https://www.technologynetworks.com/tn/news/science-is-becoming-less-disruptive-368887)
- Reported magnitudes: disruptiveness fell 91.9% (social sciences) to ~100% (physical sciences) for papers; for patents 1980–2010, 78.7% (computers & communications) to 91.5% (drugs & medical). — [Technology Networks](https://www.technologynetworks.com/tn/news/science-is-becoming-less-disruptive-368887)
- Critique (2023, University of Basel repository, "The Illusive Slump of Disruptive Patents"): "88 percent of the decrease in disruptive patents over 1980–2010 reported by the authors can be explained by their truncation of all backward citations before 1976"; after extending data to 2016 "the number of highly disruptive patents has increased since 1980, particularly in IT technologies". — [edoc.unibas.ch PDF](https://edoc.unibas.ch/95069/1/2023_06_The%20Illusive%20Slump%20of%20Disruptive%20Patents.pdf). Whether Park et al. formally responded could not be determined from available sources.

**Atypical combinations**
- Uzzi, Mukherjee, Stringer & Jones (Science 2013), 17.9 million papers: the highest-impact papers are "grounded in exceptionally conventional combinations of prior work yet simultaneously feature an intrusion of unusual combinations"; such papers were about twice as likely to be highly cited; teams were 37.7% more likely than solo authors to insert novel combinations into familiar domains. — [Kellogg PDF of Science 2013 paper](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/uzzi/htm/papers/Science-2013-Uzzi-468-72.pdf); [Kellogg summary](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2013/atypical-combinations-and-scientific-impact/)
- Follow-up (Mukherjee, Uzzi, Jones & Stringer, JPIM 2015): atypical combinations "are becoming increasingly rare with time"; novel pairings tend either to be "virtually never used again or to become conventional pairings". — [Kellogg PDF](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/uzzi/htm/papers/Mukherjee_Uzzi_Jones_Stringer-JPIM-2015.pdf)

**Risk/reward of innovative strategies**
- Foster, Rzhetsky & Evans (ASR 2015), chemical-relationship networks in biomedicine: >60% of papers introduce no new connections; "research following a risky strategy is more likely to be ignored but also more likely to achieve high impact"; "the additional reward is insufficient to compensate for the additional risk"; prize-winning (incl. Nobel) authors "introduced new molecules and relationships much more frequently". — [UChicago Knowledge Lab project page](https://voices.uchicago.edu/knowledgelab/projects/tradition-and-innovation-in-scientists-research-strategies); [ScienceDaily press release](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2015/10/151009155546.htm)
- Wang, Veugelers & Stephan (Research Policy 2017; NBER WP 22180): novelty = first-ever combinations of referenced journals. Novel papers show "delayed recognition", are "less likely to be top cited when using a short time window", but "more likely to be a top 1% highly cited paper in the long run", are cited more in "foreign" fields than their home field, show higher citation variance, and are published in lower-than-expected-impact-factor journals. — [NBER w22180](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22180)
- Chu & Evans (PNAS 2021), 1.8 billion citations among 90 million papers in 241 subjects: as a field's annual output grows, "citations flow disproportionately to already well-cited papers", new papers are unlikely to become highly cited, and canon churn decreases; proposed mechanism: the deluge "may deprive reviewers and readers of the cognitive slack needed to fully recognize and understand novel ideas". — [PMC record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8522281). A Physics Today editorial questioned whether the most-cited papers are really paradigm-shifters and whether field-level averaging produces spurious correlations. — [Physics Today editorial](https://physicstoday.aip.org/editorials/its-all-too-much)

**Kuhn**
- Normal science is "puzzle-solving" within a paradigm; an anomaly "arises when a puzzle, considered as important or essential in some way, cannot be solved"; "there is no clean line between what is an anomaly and what is merely another challenging puzzle"; failure is usually blamed on the scientist, not the paradigm; scientists "never do so [reject a paradigm] unless they have a new paradigm to replace the old one". — [LitCharts chapter summary](https://www.litcharts.com/lit/the-structure-of-scientific-revolutions/chapter-8-the-response-to-crisis); [CCSU summary](https://bertie.ccsu.edu/naturesci/Evolution/Unit10Background/Kuhn.html)
- "The Essential Tension" (1959 talk / 1977 collection): normal research is "a highly convergent activity based upon a settled consensus", yet "the ultimate effect of this tradition-bound work has invariably been to change the tradition"; the scientist must be both "a traditionalist and an iconoclast"; Kuhn warned that an exclusive focus on transformative, divergent research "may underestimate the importance of 'incremental' research marked by convergent thinking". — [Kindi, "Creativity" PDF](https://eclass-b.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/PHS269/Kindi_Creativity.pdf); [Andersen, "The Second Essential Tension"](https://philarchive.org/rec/ANDTSE); ["Transformative research focus considered harmful" (AI Magazine)](https://onlinelibrary-wiley-com-443.webvpn.zafu.edu.cn/doi/10.1002/aaai.12063)

**Lakatos**
- A theory is "theoretically progressive" if it "predicts some novel, hitherto unexpected fact", and "empirically progressive" if "some of this excess empirical content is also corroborated"; a programme that is both is progressive, "otherwise it is called a degenerating research program". Degenerating programmes handle recalcitrant data through "linguistic reinterpretations ... mere ad hoc moves" or tinker with the protective belt without novel confirmed predictions. Caveats: the "tacking problem" (one can tack on independent hypotheses to manufacture progress), progressiveness is often visible only in hindsight, and degenerating programmes can recover. — [Merritt, "Cosmology and Convention" (arXiv PDF)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.02389); [Social Research Glossary](https://qualityresearchinternational.com/socialresearch/methodologyofscientificresearchprogrammes.htm); [Oxford Phil Soc Talbot Prize essay](https://www.oxfordphilsoc.org/Documents/TalbotPrize/2020_M1.pdf)

### Inferences
- The combined empirical picture suggests a practical diagnostic: a proposal built only from the "conventional core" of a subfield (same assumptions, same tools, same neighbours in citation space) is, on base rates, a low-impact bet; a proposal that imports one atypical ingredient into an otherwise conventional frame matches the empirical profile of high-impact work (Uzzi et al.).
- Wang et al. and Foster et al. together imply that incremental work *looks* better on short-horizon metrics (citations within 3 years, impact factor), so a researcher who selects projects by expected near-term reception is systematically steered toward incrementalism. Any "escape" strategy has to deliberately discount short-horizon signals.
- Chu & Evans suggests that in very large fields (ML is one), a new idea needs to be *legibly* different to be noticed at all; a "same result, new setting" paper is especially likely to be buried.
- Lakatos gives the cleanest kill-criterion for a research *direction* (as opposed to a single project): if a line's recent papers add cases, relax assumptions, or re-interpret failures without ever predicting something new that was then checked, it is degenerating.

## Key Question 2: Which generative heuristics do strong researchers recommend?

### Takeaway
The recommended heuristics cluster into: (a) *importance tests* (Hamming: important problem + a reasonable attack; Alon: feasibility x interest; Shevlane: "best-case result, would anyone act on it?"); (b) *frontier/anomaly hunting* (Graham: gaps are only visible from the edge; a good question is a partial discovery; Nanda: taste = judging which anomalies merit exploration; Olah: humility, don't guess a priori); (c) *minimal-model and resource-scaling moves* (Olah's 2-feature/1-dimension toy model; Barak's "how performance depends on resources"); (d) *goal-driven rather than idea-driven selection* (Schulman); (e) *information-rate sequencing* for execution (Steinhardt).

### Cited Findings

**John Schulman, "An Opinionated Guide to ML Research" (2017/2020)**

**Jacob Steinhardt, "Research as a Stochastic Decision Process" (2018)**

### Inferences
- A compact "generative checklist" supported by the above: (1) Hamming test — is there a *reasonable attack* on a problem the field agrees is important? (2) Graham test — am I at the frontier, and is my puzzle one *nobody else understands*? (3) Shevlane test — if the best-case result arrives, who changes what they do? (4) Olah/Toy-model move — what is the smallest setting in which the phenomenon can be *fully* understood or analytically solved? (5) Barak move — which *resource* (data, compute, memory, context length, depth, precision) is the phenomenon really about, and what happens if a different one is scaled? (6) Nanda bound — explore freely but set an aggressive (~2 week) checkpoint with an objective test of whether anything real was found.
- Schulman's goal-driven framing is the most direct antidote to "X but with transformers": a goal-driven project is defined by a capability/explanation that does not yet exist, whereas an idea-driven project is defined by the method, which naturally generates "apply method to new setting" papers.

## Key Question 3: In ML theory (feature learning, ICL, scaling, emergence), what distinguished influential papers from "same result, new setting" papers? Case analyses

### Takeaway
Across the cases, influential papers (i) posed a question whose answer *changes a belief* the field held (emergence is real vs. a metric artifact; learning is sudden vs. hidden-but-continuous; bigger is always better vs. inverse/U-shaped scaling), (ii) picked a *minimal* setting where the answer could be made exact (sparse parity, single-index models, modular addition, 2-feature toy), and (iii) produced a *quantitative handle* (an exponent, a progress measure, a circuit, a complexity class) that others could reuse and test. "New setting" papers typically supply only (ii) without (i) or (iii).

### Cited Findings

**Case A — Schaeffer, Miranda & Koyejo, "Are Emergent Abilities of LLMs a Mirage?" (NeurIPS 2023 oral)**
- Question: is emergence a property of the model or of the metric? Claim: "emergent abilities appear due to the researcher's choice of metric rather than due to fundamental changes in model behavior with scale"; "nonlinear or discontinuous metrics produce apparent emergent abilities, whereas linear or continuous metrics produce smooth, continuous predictable changes". — [NeurIPS 2023 oral page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/oral/73863); [Stanford HAI coverage](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ais-ostensible-emergent-abilities-are-mirage)
- Method: a simple mathematical model plus three tests — predictions on the InstructGPT/GPT-3 family, a meta-analysis of BIG-Bench, and *inducing* apparent emergence in vision tasks by metric choice. Of 29 BIG-Bench metrics, 25 showed no emergence; "Multiple Choice Grade" produced the most apparent emergence; "exact string match" makes even simple arithmetic look emergent. — [Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ais-ostensible-emergent-abilities-are-mirage); [ar5iv copy of paper](https://ar5iv.org/html/2304.15004)
- Reception/critique: Jason Wei's reply ("Common arguments regarding emergent abilities") restates the metric argument in its strongest form (hard metrics like exact match don't award partial credit; log-prob improves smoothly) before responding — the response itself could not be retrieved. — [Wei blog (unreachable; title confirmed via search)](https://www.jasonwei.net/blog/common-arguments-regarding-emergent-abilities). A 2025 survey argues the mirage paper's claim that its model "qualitatively match[es]" trends "lacks quantitative rigor (e.g., no error bounds or statistical tests)". — [Emergent Abilities survey (arXiv 2503.05788)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.05788). Du et al. argue that "even continuous representations can contain a practically significant 'jump'" when read against task baselines. — [same survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.05788). A commenter's counterpoint: some capabilities are "inherently all-or-nothing, and we haven't yet found surrogate metrics which increase smoothly". — [GreaterWrong comment](https://greaterwrong.com/posts/o3r2Qru84Rz5LsQk9/are-emergent-abilities-of-large-language-models-a-mirage/comment/LG9wBTpCw9bYDRcbg)

**Case B — Michaud, Liu, Girit & Tegmark, "The Quantization Model of Neural Scaling" (NeurIPS 2023)**
- Question: can one mechanism explain both smooth power-law loss curves *and* sudden new abilities? Quantization Hypothesis: knowledge comes in discrete "quanta"; if quanta are learned in order of use frequency and frequencies follow a power law, loss follows a power law. Tested on toy data, then on LLMs by gradient-based clustering of skills, finding the frequency distribution "roughly follows a power law corresponding with the empirical scaling exponent" — described by the authors as tentative. — [arXiv 2303.13506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13506v3); [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/5b6346a05a537d4cdb2f50323452a9fe-Abstract.html)

**Case C — Olsson et al., "In-context Learning and Induction Heads" (Anthropic, 2022)**
- Question: is there a concrete circuit responsible for in-context learning, and does its formation explain the loss curve? Finding: transformers "undergo an abrupt phase transition to learning induction heads", emerging "around the same time as a notable phase change in the loss"; checks: the window "doesn't appear to correspond to a scheduled change in learning rate, warmup, or weight decay", and a different dataset "produced the same phase change pattern". Framing: interpretability as a tool to "identify which capabilities might emerge as models scale". — [arXiv 2209.11895](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.11895); [alphaXiv overview](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2209.11895v1)
- Later qualifications: one 2025 paper finds the single induction-head phase change "can only account for ICL when the answer is included within the context", and that meta-learning ability "contrasts with the single-phase change". — [arXiv 2511.01033](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.01033)

**Case D — Grokking: Power et al. (2022) → Barak et al. "Hidden Progress" (NeurIPS 2022) → Nanda et al. "Progress measures" (ICLR 2023)**
- Power, Burda, Edwards, Babuschkin & Misra: on small algorithmic datasets test accuracy rises from chance to perfect "well past the point of overfitting"; "smaller datasets require increasing amounts of optimization for generalization"; generalization is "clearly decoupled from performance on the training set... in a significantly more pronounced way than ... natural data"; experiments "can be quickly reproduced on a single GPU". First shown at an ICLR 2021 workshop. — [arXiv 2201.02177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.02177v1); [Gwern mirror PDF](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/ai/nn/fully-connected/2021-power.pdf)
- Barak, Edelman, Goel, Kakade, Malach & Zhang: k-sparse parity of n bits, "a canonical discrete search problem that is statistically easy but computationally hard"; architectures learn it with n^O(k) examples and loss "abruptly drop[s] after n^O(k) iterations", "nearly match[ing] known SQ lower bounds"; the mechanism is not Langevin-like random search but "SGD gradually amplifies the sparse solution via a Fourier gap in the population gradient, making continual progress that is invisible to loss and error metrics". — [arXiv 2207.08799](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08799); [NeurIPS 2022 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2022/poster/54049)
- Nanda, Chan, Lieberum, Smith & Steinhardt: fully reverse-engineer modular addition (discrete Fourier transforms and trig identities turning addition into rotation); grokking "arises from the gradual amplification of structured mechanisms in the weights, followed by the later removal of memorizing components"; positions itself against Barak et al., whose progress measure was "heuristically defined", by *discovering* progress measures empirically; claims to show "it is possible to fully reverse engineer trained networks, and that doing so can be invaluable to understanding their training dynamics". — [arXiv 2301.05217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05217v3); [ICLR 2023 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11385)
- Counter-example of a deflationary minimal model: "Grokking in Linear Estimators – A Solvable Model that Groks without Understanding" (ICLR 2024) argues late generalization "may not imply a transition from 'memorization' to 'understanding', but can simply be an artifact of the accuracy measure". — [ICLR 2024 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2024/poster/19036)

**Case E — Feature-learning exponents: information exponent → leap → generative exponent**
- Ben Arous, Gheissari & Jagannath (JMLR 2021): "the simplest version of SGD, namely online SGD, from a random start" in high dimension; sample thresholds for consistent estimation are polynomial in d with exponent set by the "information exponent" of the population loss, with no convexity or uniform derivative bounds; "most of the data goes toward an initial search phase that builds nontrivial correlation with the ground truth", then descent is fast. Applications: phase retrieval, GLMs, online PCA, spiked tensors, single-layer networks. — [JMLR 22(106)](https://jmlr.org/papers/v22/20-1288.html); [UC Davis seminar abstract](https://statistics.ucdavis.edu/events/seminar-042121-jagannath)
- Follow-ons: d^k samples suffice and are tight for online SGD (as cited by later work); a "sequence information exponent" for attention models (arXiv 2506.02651); data repetition lets SGD beat the one-pass information-exponent barrier (arXiv 2405.15459); for non-Gaussian inputs the exponent "is no longer an informative measure" (arXiv 2602.17876). — [arXiv 2506.02651](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.02651); [arXiv 2405.15459](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15459); [arXiv 2602.17876](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.17876)
- Abbe, Boix-Adserà & Misiakiewicz: merged-staircase property is "necessary and nearly sufficient" for SGD learning of sparse Boolean functions on 2-layer mean-field nets (COLT 2022) — motivated by the fact that "no tight characterization existed for non-linear but regular networks"; then "leap complexity" (COLT 2023): conjecture that learning time is ~ d^max(Leap(f),2), proven for a Gaussian class, with "saddle-to-saddle" staged learning of the support, matching CSQ lower bounds. — [COLT 2022 PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v178/abbe22a/abbe22a.pdf); [COLT 2023 page](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v195/abbe23a.html)
- Damian, Pillaud-Vivien, Lee & Bruna (COLT 2024): information-theoretically Θ(d) samples suffice, but SQ and low-degree-polynomial algorithms need Ω(d^{k*/2}) with a new "generative exponent" k*, tight via a partial-trace algorithm, giving "a sharp computational-to-statistical gap whenever k* > 2"; they construct smooth, Lipschitz, deterministic targets with arbitrarily large generative exponents. — [PMLR v247 Damian et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v247/damian24a.html); [arXiv 2403.05529](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05529v1)

**Case F — Expressivity/impossibility: Merrill & Sabharwal, "The Parallelism Tradeoff" (TACL 2023)**
- Log-precision transformers are simulable by constant-depth logspace-uniform threshold circuits (TC0); if L ≠ P, "transformers cannot even accurately solve linear equalities or check membership in an arbitrary context-free grammar"; the result "intuitively emerges from the transformer architecture's high parallelizability"; speculative generalization: "any model architecture as parallelizable as the transformer will obey limitations similar to it" — "a potential inherent weakness of the scaling paradigm". Later: exact characterization as FO-uniform TC0 with padding, NC with polylog looping (2025); chain-of-thought as the contrast that trades parallelism for sequential power. — [arXiv 2207.00729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00729v2); [TACL PDF](https://preview.aclanthology.org/fix_video/2023.tacl-1.31.pdf); [arXiv 2505.18948](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2505.18948)

**Case G — Falsifying "bigger is better": Inverse Scaling Prize; Chinchilla**
- McKenzie et al. (TMLR 2023): 11 datasets from a public contest show inverse scaling; four proposed causes (memorised-sequence preference over in-context instructions, imitation of undesirable data patterns, easy distractor tasks, misleading few-shot demos); conclusion that "increased model scale alone may not lead to progress". Wei, Tay & Le (EMNLP 2022) re-tested with PaLM up to 540B: only four of eleven kept inverse scaling; six showed U-shaped scaling. — [FAR.AI publication page](https://far.ai/publication/mckenzie2023inverse/); [arXiv 2306.09479](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09479v1)
- Hoffmann et al. (Chinchilla, 2022): >400 models from <70M to >16B params, 5B–400B tokens; "compute-optimal scaling requires increasing model size and training tokens in approximately equal proportions"; 70B/1.4T-token Chinchilla outperformed Gopher at the same compute. Pearce & Song (2024) attribute much of the Kaplan–Chinchilla discrepancy to Kaplan "counting non-embedding rather than total parameters, combined with their analysis being performed at small scale". — [arXiv 2406.12907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12907v1)

### Inferences
- *What the influential questions had in common.* Each is a yes/no or "which exponent?" question whose *either* answer matters: if emergence is real, safety forecasting is hard; if it is a metric artifact, scaling is predictable (Schaeffer). If SGD learns parity by random search, progress is invisible and theory is stuck; if it amplifies a Fourier gap, there is a hidden continuous quantity to measure (Barak). If the information exponent were the right complexity measure, the field would stop; the generative exponent shows it was not even the right quantity for efficient algorithms (Damian). This is the "if the answer is yes/no, does anyone care?" criterion in action.
- *Minimal models earned their place by being exact, not merely small.* Sparse parity, single-index models, modular addition and the 2-feature/1-dimension toy were chosen because lower bounds (SQ/CSQ/low-degree), closed-form losses, or full reverse-engineering were achievable in them. A "new setting" paper that is small but not exactly solvable gets neither the credibility nor the reusable handle.
- *A reusable quantitative handle is the main amplifier of influence.* The information exponent, leap, generative exponent, CD-like progress measures, TC0 membership and "20 tokens per parameter" all became vocabulary that later papers had to position against — the Uzzi "atypical ingredient that becomes conventional" pattern.
- *Deflationary minimal models are a legitimate escape route.* "Grokking without understanding" (linear estimators) and "mirage" both derive their value from shrinking a celebrated phenomenon to a mechanism-free artifact; this is the inverse of the "build a mechanism" strategy, and the field rewarded both.
- *Contest-as-method* (Inverse Scaling Prize) is a way to hunt anomalies at scale when a single lab cannot; the Wei/Tay/Le U-shape follow-up shows that falsifications themselves should be re-tested at the next scale.
- Negative/impossibility results (Merrill & Sabharwal) landed because they were tied to an architectural *reason* (parallelism) and offered a testable escape hatch (chain-of-thought / looping), not just a bound.

### Gaps
- No author retrospectives or interviews on *why they chose* the questions were found for any of the cases (Schaeffer/Koyejo, Michaud, Olsson, Nanda, Ben Arous, Damian, Abbe). Nanda has publicly said (2025) that some high-profile mech-interp results were "wrong or significantly weaker than originally claimed" and that the most ambitious vision of the field is "probably dead" — [LongtermWiki profile (secondary)](https://www.longtermwiki.com/wiki/neel-nanda) — but no grokking-specific retrospective was located.
- Jason Wei's actual rebuttal content to the metric argument could not be retrieved.
- Citation counts / influence metrics for the case papers were not retrieved; "influential" is inferred from oral/spotlight status, follow-on literature naming the concepts, and press coverage.

## Key Question 4: How do good researchers decide to kill or pivot a project?

### Takeaway
The documented methods are: Steinhardt's information-rate ordering (do the cheapest, most-likely-to-fail step first so the project dies early or earns confidence), the "de-risking vs. execution" mode split, Nanda's hard time-box with an objective test, Shevlane's best-case-relevance test, Klein's pre-mortem adapted to studies ("design so that a null result still explains why"), Hamming's "do you still have an attack?", and Lakatos' degenerating-programme test for whole directions. I found no source using the exact phrase "what result would make this paper boring"; the closest documented formulations are Shevlane's best-case test and the study-premortem.

### Cited Findings
- Steinhardt: sort components "from most informative per unit time to least"; "prioritize tasks that are more likely to fail... and tasks that take less time, so we've wasted less time if one fails and get information more quickly"; two ways to estimate informativeness (expected time saved; failure rate). — [stafforini summary (secondary)](https://stafforini.com/works/steinhardt-2018-research-as-stochastic/)
- A commenter's gloss on Steinhardt: be either in "de-risking mode (determining if the project is promising as quickly as possible) OR execution mode (assuming the project is promising and trying to do it quickly)"; and ask "why a project failed, so you can tell whether the implementation was flawed or the approach itself is unpromising". — [EA Forum comment](https://ea.greaterwrong.com/posts/jfHPBbYFzCrbdEXXd/how-to-succeed-as-an-early-stage-researcher-the-lean-startup/comment/fBEdDkGK5Jtw5hPC8)
- Shevlane: "imagine the best-case scenario... Is that conclusion going to be relevant to anyone?"; borrow startup practice of "testing minimal versions of the product, getting feedback... and making dramatic changes where necessary." — [EA Forum post](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/jfHPBbYFzCrbdEXXd/how-to-succeed-as-an-early-stage-researcher-the-lean-startup)
- Nanda: exploration time-boxed to "the aggressive two weeks", then "find some objective task to test whether the curiosity found something real"; his Explore/Understand/Distill series explicitly covers "when to give up or pivot". — [FAR.AI session](https://www.far.ai/events/sessions/neel-nanda-our-pivot-to-pragmatic-interpretability); [Explore/Understand/Distill (mirror)](https://forum.nunosempere.com/posts/hmBPqApDXvhLzbiFt/how-i-think-about-my-research-process-explore-understand)
- Nanda's own field-level pivot (Dec 2025): from ambitious reverse-engineering to work "that can be empirically validated on today's models", measuring progress by "empirical feedback on proxy tasks"; a reviewer warns of Goodhart drift of proxy tasks from the North Star. — [FAR.AI](https://www.far.ai/events/sessions/neel-nanda-our-pivot-to-pragmatic-interpretability); [LessWrong review](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/fLrn5tSs8wMznFi3X/balancing-rigor-and-utility-a-review-of-a-pragmatic-vision)
- Pre-mortem (Gary Klein, HBR): at project start, assume the project "has died" and work backwards to how; it elicits "a freer, more candid discussion about things that could derail the project". Study-premortem variant (Behavioral Scientist): design a study so that "if it fails, it still explains why", since published nulls rarely show "whether the theory was wrong or the test was flawed". — [DuraSpace copy of Klein's HBR piece](https://wiki.duraspace.org/download/attachments/104563925/ProjectPremortem.pdf?api=v2); [Behavioral Scientist, "The Study Premortem"](https://behavioralscientist.org/the-study-premortem-why-publishing-null-results-is-only-the-first-step/)
- Hamming: a problem is important only while "you have a reasonable attack". — [Matt Rickard](https://mattrickard.com/working-on-the-right-problems)
- Lakatos: abandon programmes that have "entered a degenerative state", i.e., that only re-interpret anomalies ad hoc without corroborated novel predictions; note that programmes "can recover". — [Social Research Glossary](https://qualityresearchinternational.com/socialresearch/methodologyofscientificresearchprogrammes.htm)
- Hamming on the cost of not pivoting: closed-door researchers find "10 years later... all the hard work you do is sort of tangential in importance". — [Olshansky summary](https://olshansky.substack.com/p/you-and-your-research?open=false)

### Inferences
- A project-level kill protocol consistent with the sources: (1) before starting, write the best-case headline and name who would act on it (Shevlane); (2) list the ways it dies and make sure a null result would discriminate "theory wrong" from "test flawed" (study premortem); (3) order steps by information per unit time and run the cheapest likely-to-fail step first (Steinhardt); (4) set a hard checkpoint (~2 weeks for exploratory work) with a pre-specified objective test (Nanda); (5) at the checkpoint ask whether you still have a reasonable attack (Hamming) and whether the recent moves were ad hoc patches (Lakatos). Kill if (1) fails, if (2) cannot be arranged, or if (4)–(5) come back negative.
- The sources treat "kill" and "pivot" asymmetrically: Kuhn's point that paradigms are only abandoned when a replacement exists suggests, at the direction level, that one should carry a candidate replacement question before killing a line, otherwise the natural response is to keep patching.

### Gaps
- No source containing the phrases "pre-mortem" + "boring result" or "what result would make this paper boring" was found; the catalogue item is reconstructed from adjacent advice.
- Nanda's "Key Mindsets: Truth-Seeking, Prioritisation, Moving Fast" post likely contains his "is it real?" heuristics but its body could not be retrieved. — [post listing (mirror)](https://forum.nunosempere.com/posts/igS3T3QG6i7iLdptn/my-research-process-key-mindsets-truth-seeking)

## Key Question 5: Explicit advice on avoiding "X but in-context" / "Y but with transformers" incrementalism

### Takeaway
No source was found that addresses this exact pattern by name. The nearest documented material is: (a) a 2024 catalogue of questionable ML practices naming "superfluous cogs" and "modifying a SOTA model without significantly improving it" as a cheap route to nominal novelty; (b) the least-publishable-unit / salami-slicing literature; (c) Schulman's goal-driven framing; (d) Uzzi et al.'s finding that impact comes from importing an *atypical* ingredient into a conventional frame (a port of a known analysis to a new architecture is a *conventional* combination within the subfield); (e) Lakatos' novel-prediction criterion; and (f) Shevlane's observation that for beginners extending an existing line is nonetheless the right default.

### Cited Findings
- "Questionable practices in machine learning" (arXiv 2024): "superfluous cogs" — an extra module "has no effect on model performance, which nonetheless is publishable since it may match SOTA"; "one simple route to novelty is modifying a SOTA model without significantly improving it". — [arXiv 2407.12220](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.12220)
- Least publishable unit: "the minimum amount of information that can be used to generate a publication in a peer-reviewed venue", used as "a joking, ironic, or derogatory reference to the strategy of artificially inflating the quantity of one's publications"; salami publishing = one project's data "separately reported (wholly or in part) in multiple end publications". — [Wikipedia, Least publishable unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_publishable_unit)
- A theoretical-physics-flavoured "Least Interesting Unit" critique: work "not entirely uninteresting, but just interesting enough to pursue", and chasing many such units is itself a bad habit. — [Atlas of Science](https://atlasofscience.org/a-new-example-of-what-not-to-do-in-science/)
- Schulman recommends goal-driven over idea-driven research (see KQ2). — [PDF mirror](https://pengsida.net/files/John_Schulman_How_to_do_research.pdf)
- Uzzi et al.: impact comes from "exceptionally conventional combinations of prior work" plus "an intrusion of unusual combinations". — [Science 2013 PDF](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/uzzi/htm/papers/Science-2013-Uzzi-468-72.pdf)
- Shevlane: for early-career researchers, "extend a line of inquiry that existing research has begun. Read existing literature and think: can I add anything to this?" — [EA Forum](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/jfHPBbYFzCrbdEXXd/how-to-succeed-as-an-early-stage-researcher-the-lean-startup)
- Counter-evidence that some "X in setting Y" work is non-incremental: the extension of the information exponent to "sequence single-index models and single-layer attention networks" introduces a *new* quantity (sequence information exponent) rather than re-deriving the old one. — [arXiv 2506.02651](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.02651)

### Inferences
- A workable discriminator for "X but with transformers" proposals, assembled from the sources: the port is non-incremental only if at least one of the following holds — (i) a *new quantity or regime* appears that did not exist in X (Lakatos' excess content; e.g., sequence information exponent, generative exponent vs information exponent); (ii) the transfer *breaks* the original result in an informative way (Merrill–Sabharwal: parallelism changes the complexity class; data repetition beats the one-pass exponent); (iii) the new setting makes the phenomenon *exactly solvable* where it previously was not (Toy Models; sparse parity); or (iv) the result *falsifies* a belief held about the new setting (mirage; inverse scaling). If none holds, the proposal matches the "superfluous cog"/LPU profile.
- Diagnostic signs of a "meh" proposal, each traceable to a cited pattern: the headline can be stated as "we extend [method] to [setting]" with no new quantity (Lakatos/ad hoc); the best-case result changes nobody's decision (Shevlane); every ingredient is from the subfield's conventional core (Uzzi); the result would be invisible among the subfield's canon (Chu & Evans); the setting is small but not exactly solvable (contrast Olah/Barak); no one outside the home subfield would cite it (Wang et al. foreign-field citation signal); the direction's recent papers only relax assumptions or add cases (Lakatos degenerating); you are not puzzled, or others already understand it (Graham).

### Gaps
- No ML-theory-specific essay, reviewer guideline, or talk explicitly discouraging "X-but-in-context"/"Y-but-with-transformers" papers was found; the catalogue entry is an inference from general sources.
- No quantitative study of how often such extension papers appear or how they are cited in ML venues was found.
